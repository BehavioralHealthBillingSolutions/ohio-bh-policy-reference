## Rule Text

(A) For the purpose of Ohio department of medicaid (ODM) reimbursement, substance use disorder (SUD) treatment services will be defined by and provided according to the American society of addiction medicine also known as the ASAM treatment criteria for addictive, substance related and co-occurring conditions for admission, continued stay, discharge, or referral to each level of care (LOC).

(B) ODM will reimburse for two SUD assessment types, level of care and treatment planning, separately from a psychiatric diagnostic evaluation, for ASAM levels of care 1, 2.1, and 2.5.

(1) Level of care assessment. The level of care assessment is a full evaluation of the ASAM dimensional criteria completed at admission and at any transition between levels of care. The purpose of this assessment is to identify clinical needs, strengths, risks, and barriers relevant to level-of-care placement and to evaluate appropriateness for transfer or discharge based on ASAM criteria. This assessment includes the development or update of an individualized treatment plan that includes level of care recommendation and measurable goals and interventions.

(2) Treatment planning assessment. The SUD treatment planning assessment is used to measure a recipient's progress within a level of care and is completed at regular intervals determined by program standards or when clinically indicated. This assessment includes evaluation of the recipient's response to treatment interventions, treatment progress, and treatment barriers, and utilizes validated measurement tools, when appropriate, to assess change over time. The recipient's record will include documentation of the assessment results and associated treatment plan updates.

(C) ODM will reimburse for the services provided under the following ASAM levels of care:

(1) LOC 1: outpatient services. LOC 1 services are designed to treat the recipient's level of clinical severity and function. These services may be delivered in a variety of settings. Addiction, mental health, or general health care treatment personnel provide professionally directed screening, assessment, evaluation, treatment, and ongoing recovery and disease management services. Such services are provided in regularly scheduled sessions and follow a defined set of policies and procedures or medical protocols. Service provision is limited to less than nine hours per week for adults and less than six hours per week for adolescents.

(2) LOC 2: intensive outpatient or partial hospitalization programs including LOC 2 withdrawal management (WM) LOC 2 services will be capable of meeting the complex needs of people with addiction and co-occurring conditions. SUD group counseling per diem services include all SUD group counseling delivered that day whether rendered continuously or in intervals throughout the day. If services are rendered non-continuously, any breaks should be documented in the recipient's record and the time elapsed to accommodate the break should not be counted toward the total billable service time. Services can occur during the day, before or after work or school, in the evening, and on weekends.

(a) LOC 2.1 intensive outpatient (IOP) is nine to nineteen hours of SUD group counseling service per week (six to nineteen hours for adolescents).

(b) Effective July 1, 2027, the minimum length of service necessary to receive reimbursement for one hundred per cent of the applicable IOP group counseling per diem rate is three hours and one minute. If a recipient is unable to attend a full session, but participates for at least one hour and thirty one minutes, the rendering provider may be reimbursed at fifty per cent of their provider type SUD IOP per diem rate.

(c) LOC 2.5 partial hospitalization program (PHP) is twenty or more hours of SUD group counseling service per week.

(d) Effective July 1, 2027, the minimum length of service necessary to receive reimbursement for one hundred per cent of the applicable PHP group counseling per diem rate is four hours and one minute. If a recipient is unable to attend a full session, but participates for at least two hours and one minute, the rendering provider may be reimbursed at fifty per cent of their provider type SUD PHP per diem rate.

(e) LOC 2 withdrawal management services are rendered by medical and nursing professionals who provide evaluation, withdrawal management and referral services. This level of care supports the management and easing of the physiological and psychological features associated with withdrawal and is inclusive of the following services:

(i) Ongoing monitoring of symptoms and assessment of progress;

(ii) Pharmacological support to manage the signs and symptoms of intoxication and withdrawal;

(iii) Presumptive urine drug screening, including specimen collection and analysis;

(iv) Venipuncture for lab testing:

(v) Non-medical support including referral to case management, counseling and involvement in community recovery support groups;

(vi) Discharge planning as described in paragraph (E)(4) of this rule that includes comprehensive education on the continuum of substance use disorder treatment, including medications for opioid use disorder.

(3) LOC 3: Residential services/inpatient services including withdrawal management. These services are co-occurring capable, co-occurring enhanced, and complexity capable in nature and provided by certified or licensed addiction treatment, mental health and general medical personnel in a twenty-four-hour treatment setting. Services are provided in Ohio department of behavioral health (DBH) certified permanent facilities which are staffed twenty four hours a day. The services listed in paragraphs (C)(3)(a) to (C)(3)(g) of this rule are included in the residential treatment service and will not be reimbursed separately:

(a) Initial and ongoing assessments including diagnostic evaluations., SUD level of care assessments, and SUD treatment planning assessments;

(b) Crisis intervention.

(c) Individual, group, and family psychotherapy and counseling.

(d) SUD case management.

(e) Behavioral health peer recovery services.

(f) Presumptive urine drug screen specimen collection and analysis;

(g) Basic medical services.

(h) The following services are eligible for reimbursement for recipients in residential treatment:

(i) Therapeutic behavioral services.

(ii) Psychosocial rehabilitation.

(iii) Mental health day treatment.

(iv) Assertive community treatment.

(v) Intensive home based treatment.

(i) ODM will only reimburse a provider who is not directly affiliated with, or a staff member of, the residential treatment program for a service outside the SUD residential per diem for an essential medically necessary service that could not be delayed until discharge. Examples include, but are not limited to:

(i) Psychiatry for the treatment of a co-occurring mental health disorder;

(ii) Medication-assisted treatment.

(iii) Specialized health care for treatment of an acute or chronic health condition outside a residential provider's scope of practice;

(iv) Definitive urine drug testing at an offsite laboratory when necessary to finalize assessment and guide treatment decisions;

(v) Or other necessary medical treatment that is outside the scope of the residential level of care as defined by ASAM.

(j) All treatment services, regardless of provider type or provider agency, will be documented in the recipient's treatment plan maintained by the residential treatment provider. If the residential treatment provider initiated the referral for an outside service, this will be documented in the medical record along with justification for the referral.

(k) Withdrawal management services in the residential level of care will include components identified in paragraph (B)(2)(e) of this rule and will be provided within a twenty four hour supervised setting.

(l) The entity providing a residential service will ensure that the recipient has access to the appropriate provider for receipt of clinical services as stated in the ASAM treatment criteria.

(m) Eligible practitioners of substance use disorder treatment services will meet all applicable criteria stated in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code. Qualified mental health specialists are not eligible to be a residential treatment team practitioner.

(D) For SUD services at all levels of care, the medical record will substantiate the medical necessity and individualization of services rendered. Providers will adhere to documentation criteria set forth in rules 5160-1-27 and 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code.

(E) Documentation of SUD services will reflect adherence to ASAM guidelines, as evidenced by the following:

(1) The recipient's medical record will substantiate clinical justification for level of care placement and include documentation of ASAM dimensional risk ratings, individualized treatment goals linked to those dimensions, and evidence of outcomes-based care.

(2) Providers will record baseline and interval scores using validated instruments appropriate to the level of care including but not limited to the "Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment of Alcohol Scale - Revised (CIWA-Ar)" for alcohol withdrawal, "Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale (COWS)" for opioid withdrawal, "Patient Health Questionnaire - 9 (PHQ-9)" for depression, or "General Anxiety Disorder - 7 (GAD-7)" for anxiety.

(3) Progress notes will demonstrate linkage between services delivered, dimensional needs, or measurable outcomes.

(4) Transfer or discharge readiness should be assessed on an ongoing basis through review of the recipient's progress, or lack thereof, toward meeting their individualized treatment plan goals and reviewed in comparison to the ASAM dimensions for the level of care placement.

(F) Limitations.

(1) Twelve step or other self-led recovery support programs are not covered services nor reimbursable by ODM.

(2) For withdrawal management services, if there is a step-up or step-down to a different withdrawal management level of care, the consecutive billing per day by the same organizational provider is counted as a single event.

(3) Limitations for SUD assessment and services at all levels of care will be described on the the appendix to rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code, which is also posted to WWW.Medicaid.Ohio.gov as "ODM Master List for Prior Authorization." Limitations may apply to initiation and continuation of services, frequency, scope, and duration of services, and allowance for services to be provided on the same day or during the same episode of care.

(4) The following limitations apply specifically to LOC 2 services:

(a) Additional SUD group counseling will not be reimbursed on the same day as SUD IOP or PHP.

(b) For recipients of IOP, if, after the first four consecutive weeks of treatment, the weekly average of SUD group counseling is less than an average of nine to nineteen hours, services can be reimbursed at a lower level of care not to exceed nine hours per week for adults or six hours per week for adolescents, as supported by an assessment.

(c) For recipients of PHP, if, after the first four consecutive weeks of treatment, the weekly average of SUD group counseling is less than twenty hours, services can still be reimbursed at a lower level of care not to exceed 19. 9 hours per week, as supported by an assessment.

(5) The following limitations apply specifically to LOC 3 services:

(a) Residential levels of care are mutually exclusive,

(b) When a recipient transitions between residential levels of care, the admitting organizational provider can receive per diem reimbursement on the day of admissions,

(c) SUD residential service per diem cannot be billed on the day of discharge to any level of care:

(i) If the level of care transition involves transition to a new organizational residential billing provider, the discharging residential provider can bill for unbundled services provided on the day of discharge,

(ii) If the level of care transition involves movement up or down within the same organization, the consecutive billing per day is counted as a single event.

(d) Service gaps in excess of twenty-four hours are considered a termination of one admission.
