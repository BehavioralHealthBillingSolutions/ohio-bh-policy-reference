## Rule Text

(A) For the purposes of medicaid reimbursement, therapeutic behavioral services

(TBS) are focused on psychoeducation and evidence-based interventions to decrease negative effects and symptoms of mental illness.

(1) Activities included must be intended to achieve the identified goals or objectives as set forth in the individual's treatment plan. Activities include but are not limited to the following:

(a) Treatment planning. Consultation with a licensed practitioner or other eligible provider, participating in and supporting strengths-based treatment planning which may include assisting the individual and family members or other collaterals with identifying strengths and needs. Treatment planning should occur across the episode of care and is inclusive of planning for transition to other levels of care and dischargefrom services.

(b) Identification , provision, and ongoing monitoring of strategies or treatment options and individual progress . Assisting the individual and family members or other collaterals to identify advocate for, implement, and assess effectiveness of strategies or treatment options to address the negative effects of mental illness symptoms, emotional disturbances or associated behavioral health stressors which interfere with the individual's home, interpersonal, and community functioning.

(c) Supportive counseling. Developing and providing solution - focused interventions and social, emotional and behavioral management skills training informed by evidence-based practices, including but not limited to:

(i) Cognitive behavioral therapy;

(ii) Dialectical behavior therapy;

(iii) Motivational Interviewing;

(iv) Trauma-informed care;

(v) Mindfulness;

(vi) Exposure therapy.

(d) Psychoeducation services. Instruction, training and ongoing support of and advocacy with individuals and family members or other collaterals to increase their knowledge and understanding of the individual's mental illness and associated symptoms, anticipated prognosis, treatment, rehabilitation, and necessary supports for community navigation and integration; and

(e) Crisis prevention and amelioration. Assisting the individual with effectively responding to or avoiding identified precursors or triggers that would risk their remaining in a community setting or that result in functional impairments. Activities may include, but are not limited to, assisting the individual and family members or other collaterals with identifying a potential psychiatric or personal crisis, developing a crisis management plan, and , as appropriate, seeking other supports to restore stability and functioning.

(2) Eligible providers must be a licensed or an unlicensed mental health practitioner in accordance with rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code who have at a minimum:

(a) A bachelor's or a master's degree in social work, psychology, nursing, or in related human services, or

(b) A high school diploma with a minimum of three years of relevant experience as determined by the employing agency and documented in the employee's record.

(B) For the purposes of medicaid reimbursement, psychosocial rehabilitation (PSR) assists individuals with implementing interventions outlined on a treatment plan to compensate for or eliminate functional deficits and supports skills to promote independent living in a natural community environment.

(1) Activities include:

(a) Restoration, rehabilitation, and support of functioning and implementation of routines and skills critical to remaining successful in home, school, work, and community. Activities may include:

(i) Symptom management. Assisting individuals with developing skills to minimize interference from symptoms which negatively affect daily functioning and community integration;

(ii) Independent living skills. Training and support to develop skills for completion of daily tasks necessary for independent living such as, but not limited to, budgeting, grocery shopping, personal hygiene or self-care, and managing medications;

(iii) Social skills. Improving the individual's communication, conflict resolution, and interpersonal relationship skills;

(iv) Service navigation. Providing individuals with support in learning to navigate social services and community resources;

(v) Community integration. Supporting individuals as they choose, achieve, and maintain valued roles.

(b) Treatment planning. Consultation with a licensed practitioner or other eligible provider, participating in, and supporting strengths-based treatment planning which may include assisting the individual and family members or other collaterals with identifying strengths and needs. Treatment planning should occur across the episode of care and is inclusive of planning for transition to other levels of care and discharge from services.

(c) Psychoeducational services. Ongoing instruction and training of and advocacy with individuals and family members or other collaterals to increase their knowledge and understanding of the combined impact of mental illness and functional impairment and prognosis, treatment, and rehabilitation, and necessaary supports for community navigation and integration.

(2) Eligible recipients of PSR will have a mental health diagnosis co-occurring with deficits in daily, independent, or community functioning which may be attributable to a developmental disorder, intellectual delay, cognitive impairment or decline, medical condition, other etiology.

(3) Eligible providers must be licensed or an unlicensed mental health practitioner in accordance with rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code who are appropriately supervised in accordance with rule 5160-4-02 of the Administrative Code and have at a minimum:

(a) a bachelor's or master's degree in social work, psychology, nursing, or in a related human services field, or

(b) A high school diploma and be at least eighteen years of age, and

(c) Have completed population-specific training as determined by the employing agency and documented in the employee's record.

(C) For the purposes of this rule, collateral contact occurs when the practitoner contacts individuals who play a significant role in a medicaid recipient's life. The information gained from collateral contactmust provide insight into the individual's treatment and progress.

(D) Limitations.

(1) TBS and PSR will not be reimbursed when an individual is enrolled in assertive community treatment (ACT) or receiving residential substance use disorder treatment services.

(2) Effective March 1, 2027, provision of TBS or PSR services will not be reimbursed under this rule for the provision of services which more accurately meet the description of services and activities listed in rule 5160-34-02 of the Administrative Code.

(3) TBS and PSR may be rendered in a hospital setting only for coordinating care related to an inpatient admission or when facilitating discharge.

(4) TBS must be delivered as an intervention with the individual and family members or other collaterals. TBS may be delivered in a group setting with the individual under the following conditions:

(a) Service provision is limited to less than 10.5 hours per week. Recipients in need of additional group services beyond this threshold should be evaluated for admission to mental health day partial hospitalization services as described in rule 5160-27-06 of the Adminstrative Code;

(b) Adults may receive up to one hour per day of TBS group on the same day as mental health day treatment intensive outpatient services provided there is documentation in the treatment plan differentiating the goals of these two services;

(c) The behavioral health provessional-to-recipient ratio of the group does not exceed 1:12.

(5) PSR must be delivered as an individual or group intervention with the recipient. Service delivery may also occur with family members or other collaterals for the activities described in paragraphs (B)(1)(b) and (B)(1)(c) of this rule. Group PSR encounters may not exceed a 1:6 behavioral health professional to patient ratio.

(6) TBS and PSR may be subject to limitations, which will be described on the master list on the department of medicaid website as referenced in rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code.

(E) Providers shall adhere to documentation requirements set forth in rules 5160-01-27 and 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code.
