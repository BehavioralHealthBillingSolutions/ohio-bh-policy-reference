## Rule Text

(A) For the purpose of medicaid reimbursement, substance use disorder

(SUD) treatment services shall be defined by and shall be provided according to the American society of addiction medicine also known as the ASAM treatment criteria for addictive, substance related and co-occurring conditions for admission, continued stay, discharge, or referral to each level of care (LOC).

(B) Medicaid will reimburse for the services provided under the following ASAM levels of care:

(1) LOC 1: outpatient services. LOC 1 services are designed to treat the individual's level of clinical severity and function. These services may be delivered in a variety of settings. Addiction, mental health, or general health care treatment personnel provide professionally directed screening, assessment, evaluation, treatment, and ongoing recovery and disease management services. Such services are provided in regularly scheduled sessions and follow a defined set of policies and procedures or medical protocols. Service provision is limited to less than nine hours per week for adults and less than six hours per week for adolescents.

(2) LOC 2: intensive outpatient or partial hospitalization programs including LOC 2 withdrawal management (WM) LOC 2 services has to be capable of meeting the complex needs of people with addiction and co- occurring conditions. SUD group counseling per diem services include all SUD group counseling delivered that day whether rendered continuously or in intervals throughout the day. If services are rendered non- continuously, any breaks should be documented in the recipient's record and the time elapsed to accommodate a break should not be counted toward the total billable service time. Services can occur during the day, before or after work or school, in the evening, and on weekends.

(a) LOC 2.1 intensive outpatient (IOP) requires nine to nineteen hours of SUD group counseling service per week.

(b) The minimum length of service necessary to bill the IOP group counseling per diem is three hours and one minute.

(c) LOC 2.5 partial hospitalization program (PHP) requires twenty or more hours of SUD group counseling service per week. If, after the first four consecutive weeks of treatment, the amount of service provided is less than twenty hours, services may still be reimbursed at a lower level of care not to exceed 19.9 hours per week, as supported by an assessment.

(d) The minimum length of service necessary to bill the PHP group counseling per diem is four hours and one minute.

(e) LOC 2 withdrawal management services are rendered by medical and nursing professionals who provide evaluation, withdrawal management and referral services. This level of care supports the attenuation of the physiological and psychological features of withdrawal and is inclusive of the following services:

(i) Ongoing monitoring of symptoms and assessment of progress;

(ii) Pharmacological support to manage the signs and symptoms of intoxication and withdrawal;

(iii) Presumptive urine drug screening, including specimen collection and analysis;

(iv) Venipuncture for lab testing:

(v) Non-medical support including referral to case management, counseling and involvement in community recovery support groups;

(vi) Discharge planning as described in paragraph (E)(4) of this rule that includes comprehensive education on the continuum of substance use disorder treatment, including medications for opioid use disorder.

(f) Limitations of LOC 2:

(i) SUD per diem services cannot be billed with any other behavioral health per diem service on the same day, for the same individual, by the same or different billing provider.

(ii) SUD group counseling is not permitted on the same day as IOP or PHP.

(iii) Same day reimbursement for any of the following behavioral health group services must be supported by individual assessment, meet standards of medical necessity and will be limited to no more than four fifteen-minute units, or one hour per day on the same day as the IOP or PHP group service (hourly or per diem).

(a) Mental health group psychotherapy

(b) Group therapeutic behavioral services

(c) Group behavioral health peer support

(d) Mental health day treatment

(e) Group psychosocial rehabilitation

(f) Behavioral health group nursing.

(3) LOC 3: Residential services/inpatient services including withdrawal management . These services are co-occurring capable, co- occurring enhanced, and complexity capable in nature and provided by certified or licensed addiction treatment, mental health and general medical personnel in a twenty four hour treatment setting. Services are provided in Ohio department of behavioral health certified permanent facilities which are staffed twenty four hours a day. The services listed in paragraphs (C)(3)(a) through (C)(3)(g) of this rule are included in the residential treatment service and will not be reimbursed separately:

(a) Initial and ongoing assessments including diagnostic evaluations. , SUD level of care assessments, and SUD treatment planning assessments;

(b) Crisis intervention.

(c) Individual, group, family psychotherapy and counseling.

(d) SUD case management.

(e) Behavioral health peer recovery services.

(f) Presumptive urine drug screen specimen collection and analysis;

(g) Basic medical services.

(h) The following services are considered non-covered for individuals in residential treatment:

(i) Therapeutic behavioral services.

(ii) Psychosocial rehabilitation.

(iii) Mental health day treatment.

(iv) Assertive community treatment.

(v) Intensive home based treatment.

(i) Medicaid will reimburse a provider who is not directly affiliated with, or a staff member of, the residential treatment program for a service outside the SUD residential per diem only for an essential medically necessary service that could not be delayed until discharge. Examples include, but are not limited to:

(i) Psychiatry for the treatment of a co-occurring mental health disorder;

(ii) Specialized health care provider for treatment of an acute or chronic health condition outside a residential provider's scope of practice;

(iii) Definitive urine drug test at an offsite laboratory when necessary to finalize assessment and guide treatment decisions;

(iv) Or other necessary medical treatment that is outside the scope of the residential level of care as defined by the American society of addiction medicine.

(j) All treatment services, regardless of provider type or provider agency, must be documented in the individual's treatment plan maintained by the residential treatment provider. If the residential treatment provider initiated the referral for an outside service, this must be documented in the medical record along with justification for the referral.

(k) Withdrawal management services in the residential level of care must include components identified in paragraph (B)(2)(e) of this rule and be provided within a twenty four hour supervised setting.

(l) The entity providing a residential service must ensure that the recipient has access to the appropriate provider for receipt of clinical services as stated in the ASAM treatment criteria.

(m) Eligible practitioners of substance use disorder treatment services must meet all applicable requirements stated in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code. Qualified mental health specialists are not eligible to be a residential treatment team practitioner.

(n) Limitations of LOC 3:

(i) Residential levels of care are mutually exclusive, therefore a recipient can only receive services through one level of care at a time.

(ii) During level of care transitions when a recipient receives services at multiple levels of care on the same day, payment at the new level of care begins on the day following admission;

(iii) Service gaps in excess of twenty-four hours are considered a termination of one admission;

(iv) SUD residential service cannot be billed on the same day as discharge.

(C) Medicaid will reimburse for two SUD assessment types, level of care and treatment planning, separately from a psychiatric diagnostic evaluation, for ASAM levels of care 1, 2.1, and 2.5.

(1) Level of care assessment. The level of care assessment is a full evaluation of the ASAM dimensional criteria completed at admission and at any transition between levels of care. The purpose of this assessment is to identify clinical needs, strengths, risks, and barriers relevant to level-of-care placement and to evaluate appropriateness for transfer or discharge based on ASAM criteria. This assessment includes the development or update of an individualized treatment plan that includes level of care recommendation and measurable goals and interventions.

(2) Treatment planning assessment. The SUD treatment planning assessment is used to measure a recipient's progress within a level of care and is completed at regular intervals determined by program standards or when clinically indicated. This assessment includes evaluation of the recipient's response to treatment interventions, treatment progress, and barriers, and utilizes validated measurement tools when appropriate to assess change over time. The recipient's record must include documentation of the assessment results and associated treatment plan updates.

(D) For SUD services at all levels of care, the medical record will substantiate the medical necessity and individualization of services rendered. Providers shall adhere to documentation requirements set forth in rule 5160-1-27 of the Administrative Code and rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code.

(E) Documentation of SUD services must reflect adherence to ASAM guidelines, as evidenced by the following:

(1) The patient's medical record must substantiate clinical justification for level of care placed and include documentation of ASAM dimensional risk ratings, individualized treatment goals linked to those dimensions, and evidence of outcomes-based care.

(2) Providers will record baseline and interval scores using validated instruments appropriate to the level of care including but not limited to the "Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment of Alcohol Scale - Revised (CIWA-Ar)" for alcohol withdrawal, "Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale (COWS)" for opioid withdrawal, "Patient Health Questionnaire - 9 (PHQ-9)" for depression, and "General Anxiety Disorder - 7 (GAD-7)" for anxiety.

(3) Progress notes must demonstrate linkage between services delivered, dimensional needs, and measurable outcomes.

(4) Transfer or discharge readiness should be assessed ongoing by review of the recipient's progress, or lack thereof, with the individualized treatment plan goals and reviewed in comparison to the ASAM dimensions for the level of care placement.

(F) Limitations.

(1) Twelve step or other self-led recovery support programs are not covered services nor reimbursable by medicaid.

(2) For withdrawal management services, if there is a step-up or step-down to a different withdrawal management level of care, the consecutive billing per day by the same organizational provider is counted as a single event.

(3) Limitations for SUD assessment and services at all levels of care will be described on the master list on the Ohio department of medicaid website as referenced in rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code. Limitations may apply to initiation and continuation of services, frequency, scope, and duration of services, and allowance for services to be provided on the same day or during the same episode of care.
