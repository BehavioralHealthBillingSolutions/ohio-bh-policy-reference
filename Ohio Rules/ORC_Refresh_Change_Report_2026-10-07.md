## Substantive Changes Since September 4

<a id="orc-5163-05"></a>

### ORC 5163.05 — Medicaid immigration-status eligibility

The prior page contained the former aged, blind, and disabled eligibility provision and identified it as repealed effective September 30, 2025. The current section, effective October 6, 2026 under S.B. 315, now states that an individual is not eligible for Ohio Medicaid unless the individual is eligible under 42 U.S.C. 1396b(v)(5).

**Likely impact:** Eligibility and intake teams should expect possible changes to eligibility determinations involving immigration status and should not rely on the pre-October 6 text. Coverage decisions remain the responsibility of ODM and the eligibility system; potentially affected cases warrant human review.

### ORC 5164.32 — Provider-agreement duration

Provider agreements now expire no later than **three years** after their effective date, or sooner when the Medicaid Director determines it necessary. The former statutory maximum was five years.

**Likely impact:** Revalidation calendars, credentialing work queues, and ownership/licensure monitoring should be aligned to a three-year maximum cycle. A missed revalidation can interrupt payment and, under the existing continuation language, may expose services furnished after expiration to nonpayment if revalidation is denied.

<a id="orc-5164-33"></a>

### ORC 5164.33 — Enrollment, revalidation, suspension, and heightened scrutiny

The section now expressly authorizes heightened scrutiny for high-risk providers when exclusion or termination would create access-to-care issues; denial based on ownership or co-residence relationships with suspended or terminated providers; suspension after one year with no submitted claim; and corresponding state moratoria when federal provider-enrollment moratoria apply. It also authorizes information sharing with health-care regulatory boards or commissions.

**Likely impact:** Providers should ensure address, household, ownership, affiliation, and inactivity data are accurate before enrollment or revalidation. Dormant Medicaid IDs and shared-location arrangements now carry elevated enrollment and suspension risk.

<a id="orc-5164-36"></a>

### ORC 5164.36 — Credible allegations of fraud and payment suspension

The definition now expressly includes fake check-ins, forged paperwork, double billing, identity misuse, impossible travel, claims overlapping a hospital stay when not authorized by an individual service plan, and coordinated billing rings. When the Attorney General or Auditor of State submits a credible allegation with evidence, ODM must suspend payments in whole, in part, or for targeted payments and require prepayment review, subject to federal law. The amendment also adds a hearing and independent administrative-review path for those payment suspensions and directs ODM to establish expedited appeal procedures.

**Likely impact:** This is a high operational and cash-flow risk. Agencies should monitor impossible-travel patterns, hospital-overlap claims, duplicate billing, identity use, altered check-ins, and coordinated billing anomalies before submission. Audit-ready documentation and rapid response ownership are essential.

<a id="orc-5164-57"></a>

### ORC 5164.57 — Mandatory recovery of impossible claims

ODM must recover overpayments when an audit verifies an impossible claim, including in-home care claimed while the recipient was hospitalized or simultaneous in-home services claimed for recipients at different addresses.

**Likely impact:** Agencies should add prebilling checks for inpatient overlap, overlapping staff time, conflicting locations, and travel feasibility. The statute uses mandatory recovery language once an audit verifies the condition.

<a id="orc-5167-03"></a>

### ORC 5167.03 — Medicaid MCO enrollment distribution

For eighteen months beginning October 6, 2026, ODM must ensure each participating Medicaid MCO enrolls at least ten percent of care-management-system participants.

**Likely impact:** Member-plan distribution may shift during the eighteen-month period. Agencies should monitor plan assignment, payer rosters, authorization ownership, and payer-specific billing rules when members transition.

<a id="orc-5167-18"></a>

### ORC 5167.18 — MCO reporting of program-integrity concerns

Medicaid MCOs must report credible evidence of fraud, waste, abuse, or materially inconsistent billing to ODM, and ODM must timely refer potential fraud to the Attorney General.

**Likely impact:** MCO utilization-review and payment-integrity findings can escalate beyond an ordinary claim dispute. Agencies should centralize responses and preserve the source documentation supporting services, units, time, location, rendering provider, authorization, and medical necessity.

## Newly Operative Provisions

### Effective September 23, 2026 — ORC [5119.74](#orc-5119-74) to [5119.78](#orc-5119-78)

H.B. 479 moved the State Post-Traumatic Stress Fund into Chapter 5119 and established a commission, eligibility standards, reimbursable treatment expenses, and anti-retaliation protections for public safety officers with employment-related post-traumatic stress injuries without accompanying physical injury. Reimbursement is subject to available funds, generally covers documented out-of-pocket treatment costs, and is capped at $50,000 unless additional documented expenses and available funds support a higher amount.

**Operational note:** Behavioral-health providers serving public safety officers may need to support patients with itemized treatment and payment documentation for fund applications, but the application procedure depends on commission rules.

### Effective October 6, 2026 — ORC 5164 and 5167 program-integrity package

In addition to the seven sections with newly surfaced substantive amendments described above, the following prepublished provisions are now operative:

- **Automated decisions and prior authorization:** [5164.11](#orc-5164-11) requires human review of covered automated-review determinations; [5164.12](#orc-5164-12) requires prior authorization for all therapeutic behavioral services; [5164.13](#orc-5164-13) establishes prior authorization for personal-care services exceeding the amount or scope in the plan of care or individual service plan.
- **Credentialing and enrollment:** [5164.292](#orc-5164-292) requires credentialing information every twenty-four months for listed professionals and facilities, including community mental-health, community-addiction, and Chapter 5119 residential facilities. Sections [5164.302](#orc-5164-302) through [5164.305](#orc-5164-305) add HCBS site reviews, a signed Medicaid-fraud disclaimer, standardized onboarding, and disclosure of all persons with at least five-percent direct or indirect ownership.
- **Shared identifiers and early billing spikes:** [5164.331](#orc-5164-331) requires investigation of new applicants sharing an address or phone number with a current provider. Section [5164.332](#orc-5164-332) requires payment suspension and investigation for suspicious claim increases within a new provider's first sixty days and requires flagging a monthly claim increase greater than 100 percent without a corresponding recipient increase.
- **Nonemergency medical transportation:** [5164.40](#orc-5164-40) through [5164.406](#orc-5164-406) establish GPS-based electronic verification, claim matching, fraud-detection, reporting, exemptions, and rulemaking. Section [5164.401](#orc-5164-401) requires technical standards and an implementation plan by April 6, 2027; a pilot by October 6, 2027; and use by all NEMT providers no later than April 6, 2028.
- **In-home care and EVV:** [5164.41](#orc-5164-41), [5164.42](#orc-5164-42), and [5164.421](#orc-5164-421) authorize family-caregiver oversight, make validated EVV records a condition of payment for covered services, and permit enhanced identity verification for high-risk providers. Section [5164.43](#orc-5164-43) protects employees who report suspected Medicaid fraud in good faith.
- **MCO payment integrity:** [5167.23](#orc-5167-23) permits timely prepayment review, denial, or suspension based on credible indicators, subject to ODM or Attorney General approval where specified; it also requires provider notice, grievance access, an ODM appeal route, deconfliction, and documented evidence.

## Risk Priorities for Ohio Behavioral Health and Medicaid Operations

| Priority | Risk | Immediate control |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Section 5164.12 states that all therapeutic behavioral services require prior authorization. | Confirm ODM/MCO implementation instructions by service and date; do not rely only on historical no-authorization workflows. Track authorization number, approved units, dates, rendering level, and exhaustion before billing. |
| Critical | Payment suspension, prepayment review, and mandatory recovery now explicitly target impossible travel, hospital overlap, duplicate billing, fake check-ins, identity misuse, and materially inconsistent billing. | Implement prebilling edits and a daily exception queue for time/location overlap, inpatient dates, units, EVV mismatches, duplicate encounters, rendering-provider conflicts, and abnormal volume. |
| High | Provider agreements have a three-year maximum term, while covered professionals and facilities must provide credentialing information every twenty-four months. | Maintain separate credentialing and revalidation clocks; alert at least 180, 120, 90, and 60 days before due dates. |
| High | Five-percent ownership, shared address/phone, sanctioned-owner relationships, and HCBS site-density rules can delay or block enrollment/revalidation. | Reconcile PECOS/ODM/MCO/Secretary of State ownership and location records; document legitimate co-location and shared-contact arrangements. |
| High | MCO program-integrity findings can be reported to ODM and referred to the Attorney General. | Route all MCO payment-integrity notices to one compliance owner; preserve original records and respond consistently across payers and regulators. |
| Medium | MCO enrollment distribution changes may move members across plans during the eighteen-month period. | Recheck eligibility and plan assignment near dates of service and authorization; monitor payer migration trends and denial shifts. |
| Medium | The PTSD fund creates a new reimbursement path but depends on rules and available funding. | Watch commission rulemaking; standardize itemized statements and proof-of-payment support for patients seeking reimbursement. |
