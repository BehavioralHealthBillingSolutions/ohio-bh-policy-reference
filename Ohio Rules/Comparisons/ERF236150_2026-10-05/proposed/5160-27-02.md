## Rule Text

(A) This rule sets forth coverage and limitations for behavioral health services rendered to medicaid recipients by behavioral health provider agencies who meet all obligations found in agency 5160 of the Administrative Code unless otherwise specified.

(1) All claims for behavioral health services submitted to the Ohio department of medicaid (ODM) will include an ICD-10 diagnosis of mental illness or substance use disorder. The list of recognized diagnoses can be accessed at www. medicaid.ohio.gov.

(2) ODM reimbursable behavioral health services are limited to medically necessary services defined in rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code and Chapter 5160-27 of the Administrative Code. Providers will follow the criteria in rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code and Chapter 5160-27 of the Administrative Code regarding services that cannot be billed in combination with other services.

(B) The following services may be subject to limitation on amount, scope, or duration of service that can be rendered to a recipient within a certain timeframe without authorization. Current and historical limitations may be found within the appendix to rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code, which is also posted to WWW.Medicaid. Ohio.gov as "ODM Master List for Prior Authorization."

(1) Individual and group therapeutic behavioral services (TBS) and psychosocial rehabilitation (PSR) as described in rule 5160-27-08 of the Administrative Code;

(2) Assertive community treatment (ACT) as described in rule 5160-27-04 of the Administrative Code;

(3) Mental health day treatment hourly and mental health day treatment per diem as described in rule 5160-27-06 of the Administrative Code;

(4) Behavioral health nursing as described in rule 5160-27-11 of the administrative Code;

(5) Screening, brief intervention and referral to treatment (SBIRT) as defined by the American medical association's current procedural terminology;

(6) Psychological or neuropsychological testing;

(7) Psychiatric diagnostic evaluation with or without medical services;

(8) Psychotherapy, including family, group and multiple-family;

(9) Peer support, as described in rule 5160-27-14 of the Administrative Code.

(C) The following services delivered to recipients with substance use disorders may be subject to limitations on amount, scope or duration of service that can be rendered to a recipient within a certain timeframe without authorization. Current and historical limitations may be found within the appendix to rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code, which is also posted to WWW.Medicaid.Ohio.gov as "ODM Master List for Prior Authorization."

(1) Substance use disorder assessment as referenced in rule 5160-27-09 of the Administrative.

(2) Substance use disorder urine drug screening as referenced in rule 5160-27-09 of the Administrative.

(3) Substance use disorder partial hospitalization as described in rule 5160-27-09 of the Administrative Code.

(4) Substance use disorder residential level of care as described in rule 5160-27-09 of the Administrative Code.

(5) Substance use disorder case management as described in rule 5160-27-10 of the Administrative Code.

(6) Substance use disorder withdrawal management as described in rule 5160-27- 09 of the Administrative Code.

(7) Substance use group counseling as described in rule 5160-27-09 of the Administrative Code.

(8) Substance use disorder intensive outpatient services as described in rule 5160- 27-09 of the Administrative Code.

(D) The medications listed in appendix DD to rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code, or the fee schedule located on the ODM website are covered by ODM when rendered and billed by an eligible provider as described in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code. The medication has to be administered by a qualified practitioner acting within their professional scope of practice.

(E) Laboratory services, vaccines, and medications administered in a prescriber office may be administered in accordance with rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code.

(F) Medical and evaluation and management services stated in the appendix to rule 5160-27-03 of the Administrative Code or appendix DD to rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code are covered by ODM when rendered by:

(1) A practitioner as described in paragraphs (A)(3) and (A)(4) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code and operating within their scope of practice; or

(2) A pharmacist, rendering services in accordance with rule 5160-8-52 of the Administrative Code.

(G) Centers for medicare and medicaid services (CMS) place of service (POS) code set descriptions may be found at www.cms.gov.

(1) The department further defines place of service 99 as "community," and this place of service may only be used when a more specific place of service is not available. Place of service 99 can not be used to provide services to a recipient of any age if the recipient is being held in a public institution as defined in 42 C.F.R. 435.1010 42 CFR 435.1010 (October 1, 2023) nor can it be used when the actual place of service is not permissible by CMS.

(2) Place of service 11 can only be used when the full CMS definition is met and cannot be used as a substitute when the actual POS is not permitted by CMS or ODM.

(3) Services with an allowable place of service 21, 23, and 51 can only be rendered in those locations for the purpose of supporting admission to and discharge from the hospital.

(H) The reimbursable ODM activities listed in paragraphs (B) and (C) of this rule are intended to achieve individualized goals or objectives as identified in the treatment plan and supported by an assessment. Providers will maintain treatment records and progress notes that provide support for medically necessary service provision as specified in rules 5160-01-27 and 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code.

(1) A treatment plan for mental health services may only be developed by a licensed practitioner as identified in paragraphs (D)(1) through (D)(6) of rule 5160- 27-01 of the Administrative Code. An unlicensed practitioner as defined in paragraph (A)(6)(a) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code may develop a treatment plan only under the supervision of a licensed practitioner.

(2) A treatment plan for substance use disorder services may only be developed by a practitioner who, at a minimum meets the practitioner criteria found in paragraph (A)(6)(b) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code.

(I) The medications and services listed in the appendix to rule 5160-27-03 of the Administrative Code or the opiate treatment service section of appendix DD to rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code are reimbursed by ODM when rendered and billed by an opiate treatment program as described in Chapter 5122-40 of the Administrative Code and licensed as such by the Ohio department of behavioral health or federally certified as such as stated in 42 CFR 8.11 (October 1, 2026). Reimbursement rates are determined by the methodology described in rule 5160- 4-12 of the Administrative Code or as listed in the appendix to rule 5160-27-03 of the Administrative Code or as listed in appendix DD to rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code.

(J) When permitted, provision of any service addressed in Chapter 5160-27 of the Administrative Code has to comply with the appropriate telehealth criteria found in rule 5160-1-18 of the Administrative Code. For community behavioral health providers certified by the Ohio department of behavioral health, telehealth services has to also be rendered in accordance with rule 5122-29-31 of the Administrative Code.

(K) The services described in this chapter will not substitute or supplant unpaid familial, community, vocational, or educational supports and do not include any of the following:

(1) Educational, or job training services or vocational and supported employment services unless the recipient is enrolled and receiving any of these types of services through the specialized recovery services program as described in rule 5160-43-01 of the Administrative Code.

(2) Room and board.

(3) Habilitation services including but not limited to financial management, supportive housing, and basic skill acquisition services that are habilitative in nature.

(4) Recreational and social activities, including but not limited to arts and crafts, game play, cosmetology, listening to music, watching television, movies, or other forms of visual media, and equine therapies;

(5) Meal or snack time;

(6) Clinical supervision is not reimbursable by supervisor or supervisee. Activities fall under supervision include, but are not limited to, individual or group supervision, facilitation of team or program meetings, oversight of treatment plan development, oversight of assessment, education, or training related to primary job responsibilities, activities to support professional skills development, clinical case consultation, observation, clinical documentation review.

(7) Collateral contacts with another clinician or colleague employed by the same organizational provider under the same line of business are not reimbursable. Contacts between rendering providers under the same organization but in a different line of business are reimbursable by ODM if all the following components are met:

(a) The medicaid recipient has given consent to the collateral contact and this is documented in the medical record. If the recipient is incapacitated or otherwise unable to provide consent, a statement that justifies the need for contact without consent will be documented by the initiating provider.

(b) There is a clearly stated purpose for the cross-disciplinary contact that directly relates to the recipient's treatment plan.

(c) The outcome of the contact is beneficial to the recipient served and is documented in the medical record.

(d) Concurrent reimbursement for the contact by each individual provider is prohibited.

(8) Services that are covered elsewhere in agency 5160 of the Administrative Code; and

(9) Transportation for the recipient or family unless otherwise permitted by rule 5160- 27-13 of the Administrative Code.

(L) The "Ohio children's initiative brief CANS assessment" and the "Ohio children's initiative comprehensive CANS assessment" are covered as defined in rule 5160-59-01 of the Administrative Code and may be billed separately for reimbursement. Payment for therapeutic behavioral services, psychosocial rehabilitation, psychotherapy, or psychiatric diagnostic evaluation is not allowable for provision of the Ohio brief or Ohio comprehensive CANS assessment.
