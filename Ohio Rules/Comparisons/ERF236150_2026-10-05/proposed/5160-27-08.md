## Rule Text

(A) Therapeutic behavioral services (TBS) and psychosocial rehabilitation (PSR) are an array of activities intended to provide individualized supports to eligible recipients. These activities are trauma informed and intended to achieve the identified goals or objectives as set forth in the recipient's treatment plan.

(B) For the purposes of Ohio department of medicaid (ODM) reimbursement, both TBS and PSR include the following activities:

(1) Treatment planning. Participating in and supporting strengths-based treatment planning with direct involvement of the recipient which can include assisting the recipient and family members or other collaterals with identifying strengths and needs. Treatment planning should occur across the episode of care and includes ongoing needs assessment and planning for transition to other levels of care and discharge from services.

(2) Psychoeducation services. Instruction, training ongoing support of, and advocacy for recipients and family members or other collaterals to increase knowledge and understanding of the recipient's mental illness, functional impairments, associated symptoms, anticipated prognosis, treatment, rehabilitation, and needed supports for community navigation and integration; and

(3) Crisis prevention and amelioration. Assisting the recipient with effectively responding to or avoiding identified precursors or triggers that present risk to remaining in a community setting or that result in functional impairments. Activities may include, but are not be limited to, assisting the recipient and family members or other collaterals with identifying a potential psychiatric or personal crisis, developing a crisis management plan, and, as appropriate, seeking other supports to restore stability and functioning.

(C) For the purpose of ODM reimbursement, additional activities permitted under therapeutic behavioral services (TBS) are focused on psychoeducation and evidence-based interventions to decrease negative effects and symptoms of mental illness.

(1) These activities include:

(a) Identification, provision, and ongoing monitoring of strategies or treatment options and recipient progress. Assisting the recipient and family members or other collaterals to identify advocate for, implement, and assess effectiveness of strategies or treatment options to address the negative effects of psychiatric symptoms, emotional disturbances or associated behavioral health stressors that interfere with the recipient's home, interpersonal, and community functioning.

(b) Develop and provide solution- focused interventions and social, emotional and behavioral management skills training informed by evidence-based practices and associated activities and methodologies, including but not limited to:

(i) Cognitive behavioral therapy;

(ii) Dialectrical behavior therapy;

(iii) Motivational interviewing;

(iv) Mindfulness;

(v) Exposure therapy.

(2) Eligible providers of TBS can be a licensed or an unlicensed mental health practitioner in accordance with rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code who have at a minimum:

(a) A bachelor's or master's degree in social work, psychology, nursing, or in related human services, or

(b) A high school diploma with a minimum of three years of relevant experience as determined by the employing agency and documented in the employee's record.

(D) For the purposes of ODM reimbursement, additional activities permitted under psychosocial rehabilitation (PSR) will support eligible recipients through interventions that compensate for or eliminate functional deficits and supports skills to promote independent living skills in a natural community environment.

(1) Activities include:

(a) Restoration, rehabilitation, and support of functioning and implementation of routines and skills critical to remaining successful in home, school, work, and community. Activities may include:

(i) Symptom management. Assisting recipients with developing skills to minimize interference from symptoms which negatively affect daily functioning and community integration;

(ii) Independent living skills. Training and support to develop skills for completion of daily tasks necessary for independent living such as, but not limited to, budgeting, grocery shopping, personal hygiene or self-care, and managing medications;

(iii) Social skills. Improving the recipient's communication, conflict resolution, and interpersonal relationship skills;

(iv) Service navigation. Supporting recipients in learning to navigate and maintain linkage to social services and community resources;

(v) Community integration. Supporting recipients as they choose, achieve, and maintain valued roles in their community.

(2) Eligible recipients of PSR will have a mental health diagnosis co-occurring with deficits in daily, independent, or community functioning which may be attributable to a developmental disorder, intellectual delay, cognitive impairment or decline, medical condition, or other etiology.

(3) Eligible providers of PSR can be licensed or an unlicensed mental health practitioner in accordance with rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code and have at a minimum:

(a) a bachelor's or master's degree in social work, psychology, nursing, or in a related human services field, or

(b) A high school diploma and be at least eighteen years of age, and

(c) Completed population-specific training as determined by the employing agency and documented in the employee's record.

(E) For the purposes of this rule, collateral contact occurs when the practitioner has meaningful interaction with a recipient who play a significant role in a recipient's life. The information gained from the collateral contact will provide insight into the recipient's treatment and support progress toward treatment goals.

(F) Limitations.

(1) TBS and PSR will not be reimbursed when a recipient is enrolled in assertive community treatment (ACT) or receiving residential substance use disorder treatment services.

(2) Individual TBS can be delivered as an intervention with the recipient, family members, or other collaterals.

(3) TBS may be delivered in a group setting with the recipient under the following conditions:

(a) Service provision is limited to less than 10.5 hours per week. Recipients in need of additional group services beyond this threshold will be evaluated for admission to mental health day treatment hourly or mental health day treatment per diem program as described in rule 5160-27-06 of the Administrative Code;

(b) The behavioral health professional-to-recipient ratio of the group does not exceed 1:12.

(4) PSR can be delivered as an individual or group intervention with the recipient. Service delivery may also occur with family members or other collaterals for the activities described in paragraphs (B)(1)(b) and (B)(1)(c) of this rule. Group PSR encounters may not exceed a 1:6 behavioral health professional to recipient ratio.

(5) TBS and PSR may be subject to limitations on coverage and reimbursement, which will be described on the the appendix to rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code, which is also posted to WWW.Medicaid.Ohio.gov as "ODM Master List for Prior Authorization."

(G) Providers have to adhere to documentation criteria set forth in rules 5160-01-27 and 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code.
