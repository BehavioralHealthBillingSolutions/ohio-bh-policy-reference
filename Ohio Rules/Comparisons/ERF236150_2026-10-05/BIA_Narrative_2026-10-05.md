The Common Sense Initiative is established in R.C. 107.61 to eliminate excessive and
duplicative rules and regulations that stand in the way of job creation.  Under the Common
Sense Initiative, agencies must balance the critical objectives of regulations that have an
adverse impact on business with the costs of compliance by the regulated parties. Agencies
should promote transparency, responsiveness, predictability, and flexibility while developing
regulations that are fair and easy to follow. Agencies should prioritize compliance over
punishment, and to that end, should utilize plain language in the development of regulations.

Please include the key provisions of the regulation as well as any proposed amendments.
5160-8-05:  Provides definitions and general Medicaid coverage and payment policy for behavioral
health services.  Revisions include policy and payment clarifications.
5160-27-01:  Defines the eligible providers of Medicaid covered behavioral health services.
Revisions include updating a sister agency name and correcting a typo.
5160-27-02: Coverage and limitations of behavioral health services.  States services that might be
subject to provision limits and how such limits can be  bypassed.  The CPST service  is removed
from the rule and existing policy  is clarified.
5160-27-04 (Rescind):  Mental health assertive community treatment service.  States the policy
concerning the assertive community treatment service.  This rule is being rescinded due to the
quantity of revisions being made and filed as new.
5160-27-04 (New):  Mental health assertive community treatment service.  States the policy
concerning the assertive  community treatment service.  Revisions from the rescinded rule include
removal of much of the rule text and reference 5122-29-29 as a source of service information.
5160-27-06:  Mental health intensive outpatient and partial hospitalization services.  Defines the
two services and states service requirements and coverage limitations.  Revisions include policy
clarification regarding group counseling services and treatment intensity.
5160-27-08: Mental health therapeutic behavioral services and psychosocial rehabilitation.
Describes two services, therapeutic behavioral service and  psychosocial rehabilitation and states
Medicaid coverage policy.  Revisions include policy clarification concerning service coverage
limitations and service activity components.
5160-27-09: Substance use disorder treatment services.  Describes and states the purpose of
Medicaid covered substance use disorder services and includes the reimbursement policy.
Revisions include clarification of  existing policy and new policy regarding reimbursement and
documentation requirements.
5160-27-10: Substance use disorder targeted case management.  Describes and states the coverage
limitations for the substance use disorder targeted case management service.  Revisions include
updating the name of a sister agency mentioned in the rule and updating targeted case management
information.
5160-27-14: Behavioral health peer support service. Describes the eligible providers, coverage
limitations,  and reimbursement policy for the behavioral health peer support service.  Revisions
include updating the name of a sister agency used in the rule and updating service limitation and
reimbursement policy.

15. Provide a summary of the estimated cost of compliance with the rule(s). Specifically,
please do the following:
ODM enforces requirements using rules in the Ohio Administrative Code (OAC) Chapter 25 and
rule 5160-8-05 which is specific to Medicaid covered behavioral health services.  Services are
aligned with Ohio’s Medicaid State Plan with the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services
(CMS).  A State Plan Amendment will be submitted to CMS to address any State Plan changes
needed because of these rules.
The proposed rules were reviewed by the Ohio Department of Behavioral Health (DBH) to ensure
that duplicative rules did not exist in their sets of rules but rather the proposed rules are
complementary to those from DBH.
ODM maintains, on its website, a manual that provides information regarding the behavioral health
services it covers.  It is revised on a regular basis and will be again once these rules are in effect.
ODM also maintains an electronic mailbox that providers and stakeholders can use to send email if
they have questions regarding regulations outlined in the rules.

1.  Behavioral health provider agencies that choose to render services to Medicaid
recipients must be certified by the Ohio Department of Behavioral Health (DBH).
2. Service provision authorization requirements (strengthening to support fraud, waste,
and abuse policies) may limit the provision of some services.
3. To render some specific services, a practitioner must hold a required degree, license, or
certification  in a specific field.
4. To render one specific service, assertive community treatment (ACT), a team of
practitioners must meet service fidelity requirements.
5. To receive ACT a recipient must meet specific clinical or other criteria.
6. A provider agency wishing to render and bill for ACT must hold current ACT
certification given by DBH.
7. ODM may suspend or terminate payment for ACT if the ACT practitioner team does
not meet fidelity criteria.
8. In some situations, if a practitioner must document client information, specific
documentation requirements must be met.
1.  Provider certification costs vary according to the size of the provider agency and
which services it chooses to provide thus the certification cost can vary from agency to
agency.  The cost is determined by the Ohio Department of Behavioral Health.
2. Revenue reduction from potential service provision limitations will vary significantly
among agencies depending on the quantity of services not authorized.
3. Degree, certification, or licensure costs will vary depending on the entity granting the
degree, certification, or license.
4. To meet ACT fidelity requirements, a practitioner team will work with Case Western
Reserve University to determine what preparation needs to be done and costs (if any)
involved.
5. If a recipient does not meet the medical necessity criteria to receive ACT, they could
not receive the service but would be eligible for a more appropriate clinical service.
6. The cost for a provider agency to obtain ACT certification is determined by DBH
specific provider requirements and therefore will vary among provider agencies.
7. The amount of suspended or terminated payment by ODM would vary depending on
the quantity of ACT service rendered.
8. Providing documentation will take some of the practitioner’s time but is usually a
clinical requirement.

19. How will the agency apply Ohio Revised Code section 119.14 (waiver of fines and

No proposed change in any of the rules would result in a reduction of regulatory burden.
1. Provider agency certification supports the goal that agencies are equipped and qualified to
provide appropriate and needed behavioral health services to Medicaid recipients.
2. Service provision requirements support the goal of Medicaid coverage only for
appropriate, medically necessary services as well as supporting fraud, waste, and abuse
prevention activities.
3. Practitioner requirements support the goal that individuals are equipped and qualified to
provide appropriate and needed services to Medicaid recipients.
4. Meeting fidelity requirements supports the goal that an ACT team is equipped and
qualified to provide appropriate and needed ACT services to Medicaid recipients.
5. Medical necessity for a specific service, by a Medicaid recipient, is a Medicaid
requirement.
6. Provider agency ACT certification supports the goal that agencies are equipped and
qualified to provide appropriate and needed ACT services to Medicaid recipients.
7. Reducing and eliminating fraud, waste, and abuse policies are strengthen by withholding
payment to providers who are not qualified to render ACT services.
8. Service provision documentation is good clinical policy and helps support the medical
necessity requirement.
To ensure uniform and consistent treatment of Medicaid providers, the proposed rules do not offer
any exemptions or alternate means of compliance based on a provider’s size.
This regulation does not apply to this rule package because it does not impose any fine or penalty
for a paperwork violation.
