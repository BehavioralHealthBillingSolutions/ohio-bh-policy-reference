# 5122-29-06 — Mental health day treatment service

## Rule Text

(A)  As used in this rule:

(1)  "Provider" means a provider described in rule 5122-25-01 of the Administrative Code to whom Chapter 5122-25 of the Administrative Code applies.

(2)  "Mental health day treatment program day" means the total amount of hours an individual receives mental health day treatment service during a twenty-four hour calendar day.

(B)  Mental health day treatment is an intensive, structured, goal-oriented, distinct, and identifiable treatment service that utilizes multiple mental health interventions that address the individualized mental health needs of the client. Mental health day treatment services are clinically indicated by assessment with clear admission and discharge criteria. The environment at this level of treatment is highly structured and there should be an appropriate staff-to-client ratio to guarantee sufficient therapeutic services and professional monitoring. The purpose and intent of mental health day treatment is to stabilize, increase, or sustain the highest level of functioning and promote movement to the least restrictive level of care. The outcome is for the individual to develop the capacity to continue to work towards an improved quality of life with the support of an appropriate level of care.

(C)  Mental health day treatment is an intense treatment service that consists of high levels of face-to-face mental health interventions that address an individual's mental health needs as identified in the individual's individualized treatment plan (ITP).

(D)  The minimum program length of this service is to be in accordance with the appropriate behavioral health standards of the agency's one or more national accrediting bodies. Such accrediting bodies are identified in rule 5122-25-02 of the Administrative Code.

(E)  For purposes of this rule, a mental health day treatment program day is to consist of a minimum of two hours and up to a maximum of seven hours per day, at least three days a week, of scheduled intensive activities that may include, but are not limited to, the following:

(1)  Determination of needed mental health interventions;

(2)  Skills development

(a)  Interpersonal and social competency as age, developmentally, and clinically appropriate, such as:

(i)  Functional relationships with adults;

(ii)  Functional relationships with peers;

(iii)  Functional relationships with the community/schools;

(iv)  Functional relationships with employer/family; and

(v)  Functional relationships with authority figures.

(b)  Problem solving, conflict resolution, and emotions/behavior management.

(c)  Developing positive coping mechanisms;

(3)  Managing mental health and behavioral symptoms to enhance vocational/school opportunities and/or independent living; and

(4)  Psycho-educational interventions including individualized instruction and training of persons served in order to increase their knowledge and understanding of their psychiatric diagnosis(es), prognosis(es), treatment, and recovery, improve their coping skills, and favorably affect their outcomes. Such education is to be consistent with the individual's ITP and be provided with the knowledge and support of the interdisciplinary/intersystem team providing treatment in coordination with the ITP.

(5)  That the provider, as identified in the individual's ongoing assessment, provide, directly or through appropriate referral, treatment related to medical, psychological, or psychiatric needs.

(6)  That the provider has the ability to monitor program participants for changes in areas such as symptom severity, the need to move to a higher or lower level of care, and shifts in social skills or daily functioning. This ongoing monitoring helps identify early signs of clinical decline, guide timely interventions, assess how well treatments are working, adjust the comprehensive individualized treatment plan developed under rule 5122-27-03 of the Administrative Code, and support each program participant's recovery goals by strengthening coping skills, encouraging community engagement, and promoting independence in daily living.

(F)  Providers of mental health day treatment services are to have a staff development plan based upon identified individual needs of mental health day treatment program staff. Evidence that the plan is being followed shall be maintained.

(G)  Mental health day treatment service is to be provided and supervised by an interdisciplinary team of mental health treatment staff who are qualified according to rule 5122-29-30 of the Administrative Code, and only those components of the service which are appropriate to the individual's credential or qualifications.
