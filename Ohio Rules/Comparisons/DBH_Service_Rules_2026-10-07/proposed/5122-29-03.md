## Rule Text

(A)  As used in this rule, "Provider" means a provider described in rule 5122-25-01 of the Administrative Code to whom Chapter 5122-25-01 of the Administrative Code applies.

(B)  "General services" are the assessment activities, medical activities, and counseling and therapy activities described in this rule.

(C)  General services are to be provided by the professionals specified in appendix A to this rule within the scope of practice of those professionals. If such professionals are providing general services to individuals with a substance use disorder, their delivery of the services are subject to the following:

(1)  If the services to be provided are level 2.1 services under American society of addiction medicine (ASAM) criteria, third edition, 2013, known as intensive outpatient services, the services are to be provided in accordance with that criteria.

(2)  If the services to be provided are level 2.5 services under ASAM criteria, third edition, 2013, known as partial hospital services, the services are to be provided in accordance with that criteria.

(D)  Assessment activities

(1)  Definition An assessment is an individualized, person-centered, developmentally appropriate, and culturally responsive clinical evaluation of an individual, conducted by qualified staff, that determines an individual's diagnosis and treatment needs and directly informs the development of the individual's initial and comprehensive individualized treatment plans described in rule 5122-27-03 of the Administrative Code to address the individual's primary or co-occurring mental illness and/or substance use disorder, problem gambling, tobacco or nicotine use.

(2)  Timing When an individual is to receive an assessment, it should begin before any other services are provided, unless the situation is an emergency.

(3)  Initial assessment standards An initial assessment is to include, at a minimum, an evaluation of all of the following:

(a)  The presenting problem;

(b)  The risk of harm to self and others;

(c)  The use of alcohol or drugs;

(d)  History of treatment for mental illness, substance use, problem gambling, or tobacco or nicotine use; and,

(e)  A medical history and examination (mental status or physical).

(4)  Comprehensive assessment standards A comprehensive assessment expands on the initial assessment by gathering any additional information needed to establish and implement a comprehensive treatment plan. It is to be completed within thirty days of the initial assessment encounter.

(5)  Order of assessments It is not necessary that an individual have an initial assessment before receiving a comprehensive assessment.

(6)  Standards of care Both initial and comprehensive assessments for mental health and substance use disorder are to follow prevailing standards of care and accreditation guidelines specified by:

(a)  The joint commission;

(b)  The commission on accreditation of rehabilitation facilities;

(c)  The council on accreditation;

(d)  The American society of addiction medicine (ASAM); or

(e)  Other entities as designated by the director.

(7)  Use of prior assessments A provider may accept an initial or comprehensive assessment completed by another provider within the past twelve months. The prior assessments are to be reviewed and updated as necessary.

(E)  Counseling and therapy

(1)  Definition Counseling and therapy is an interaction with an individual or group of individuals that focuses on achieving treatment objectives related to mental illness and/or substance use disorder, problem gambling, tobacco or nicotine use, and/or co- occurring conditions, as indicated.

(2)  Format Counseling and therapy involves interaction between a behavioral health professional and an individual, group of individuals, individual and family members, or family members and a behavioral health professional.

(3)  Ratio of behavioral health professionals to clients Group counseling and therapy encounters are to maintain a ratio no greater than one behavioral health professional to every twelve clients.

(F)  Medical activities.

(1)  Definition: Medical activities are those activities that meet both of the following criteria:

(a)  Are performed within the professional scope of practice by an individual who is licensed or certified by the state medical board of Ohio or the Ohio board of nursing, or who is a pharmacist licensed by the Ohio board of pharmacy; and

(b)  Are intended to address the behavioral and other physical health needs of clients receiving treatment for psychiatric symptoms or substance use disorders.

(2)  Examples Medical activities include, but are not limited to:

(a)  Performing health care screenings, assessments, and exams;

(b)  Checking vital signs;

(c)  Ordering laboratory tests and reviewing the results; and

(d)  Medication prescribing, administering, and monitoring.
