# 5122-29-16 — Peer run organization service

Status: Draft – Not for Filing; no effective date stated.

## Rule Text

(A)  As used in this rule:

(1)  "Provider" means a provider described in rule 5122-25-01 of the Administrative Code to whom Chapter 5122-25 of the Administrative Code applies.

(2)  "Recovery" means the personal process of change in which an individual strives to improve their health, wellness, and resiliency, as well as reach their full potential through self-directed actions.

(B)  Peer run organization service is comprised of both activities specified in paragraph (C)(1) of this rule and one or more activities specified in paragraph (C)(2) of this rule that are delivered by individuals, a majority of whom have direct lived experience with mental illness or a substance use disorder, that leverages the power of those individuals' shared lived experiences to promote the behavioral health recovery and well-being of other persons with mental illness or a substance use disorder.

(C)  For a provider to be certified to provide peer run organization ed service, the provider is to demonstrate all of the following:

(1)  That it is able to provide both of the following activities of the peer run organization service:

(a)  Recovery supports as defined in section 5119.01 of the Revised Code, whether occurring in an individual or group setting; and

(b)  Peer support services provided by certified peer supporters, as defined in rule 5122-29-15 of the Administrative Code, that are not delivered as part of a comprehensive individualized treatment plan described in rule 5122-27-03 or reimbursed on a fee-for service or other third-party reimbursement basis through medicaid or private insurance. Receipt by the provider of a grant, contract, appropriation, or other public funding to support peer run organization services does not constitute reimbursement for purposes of this paragraph.

(2)  That it is able to provide one or more of the following activities of the peer run organization service:

(a)  Consultation to a board of alcohol, drug addiction, and mental health services and other community agencies regarding identifying unmet recovery needs and potential solutions for fulfilling those needs;

(b)  Recovery planning;

(c)  Recovery meetings;

(d)  Support groups;

(e)  Links to resources;

(f)  Skills-building groups;

(g)  Increasing recovery supports workforce capacity through training, development, and other activities;

(h)  Harm reduction activities that may be supported with funding from the United States substance abuse and mental health services administration (including overdose prevention and response services) according to guidance from that federal agency;

(i)  Education;

(j)  Socialization activities;

(k)  Wellness activities;

(l)  Activities involving art;

(m)  Meals;

(n)  Health and fitness classes;

(o)  Structured volunteer and work activities;

(p)  Recovery-focused outreach and advocacy activities; or

(q)  Hosting of recovery celebrations.

(3)  That either of the following is true:

(a)  At least fifty-one per cent of the individual members of the provider's board of directors or other governing body self-identify as having direct, lived experience and are in recovery from a mental illness or substance use disorder; or

(b)  In the case of a provider whose primary mission is the delivery of family peer support services or services focused on the caregiver experience, at least fifty- one per cent of the individual members of the provider's board of directors or other governing body self-identify as caregivers of an individual with mental illness or a substance use disorder.

(4)  A provider must establish, publish, and generally maintain predictable hours appropriate to its service model and the needs of the community it serves.

(5)  That the provider makes the service available to all individuals in the community with mental illness or substance use disorder, not restricting the service to participants or residents of any particular housing program, treatment program, service agency, or residential facility, and that all of the following are the case:

(a)  The provider's outreach and promotional materials clearly state that the activities of the service are open to all community members;

(b)  The provider has no referral, enrollment, or eligibility standards limiting access to individuals connected to a specific housing program, treatment provider, service agency, or residential facility; and

(c)  The provider's policies and handbook do not impose restrictive eligibility criteria tied to particular housing, treatment, or service providers. For purposes of demonstrating compliance with this paragraph, a provider is to maintain outreach materials or web site statements, internal policy documents, and attendance or intake records that reflect broad community participation rather that limitation of activities to a specific housing program, treatment provider, service agency, or residential facility.

(D)  In addition to having at least one physical site as required in chapter 5122-25, a provider of peer run organization service may use one or more mobile units intended to meet the needs of underserved populations.

(1)  A mobile unit is to be a van, larger recreational vehicle, or other similar type of larger motor vehicle that is equipped with enough space for at least two adults to meet comfortably for the provision and receipt of peer support services. The van, larger recreational vehicle, or similar type of larger motor vehicle is not to be owned or rented by a member of the provider's staff, governing board, or advisory board. A mobile unit is not to be a motorcycle or bicycle.

(2)  A mobile unit is to possess a valid license plate. A copy of the unit's vehicle registration is to be on file at the provider's physical location listed on the provider's application filed under rule 5122-25-02, 5122-25-03, or 5122-25-04 of the Administrative Code.

(E)  A provider of peer run organization service that provides peer support services, as described in paragraph (C)(1)(b) of this rule, may use telehealth for the delivery of the peer support services in accordance with rule 5122-26-22 of the Administrative Code.
