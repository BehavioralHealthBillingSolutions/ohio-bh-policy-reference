## Rule Text

(A)  As used in this rule:

(1)  "Provider" means a provider described in rule 5122-25-01 of the Administrative Code to whom Chapter 5122-25 of the Administrative Code applies.

(2)  "Collateral contact" occurs when the provider contacts individuals who play a significant role in the service recipient's life.

(B)  Psychosocial rehabilitation (PSR) service Psychosocial rehabilitation (PSR) service is a service comprised of one or more of the mental health activities specified in paragraph (C) of this rule. For each individual who receives the service, the service is to be tailored to that individual's needs consistent with their comprehensive individualized treatment plan developed under rule 5122-27-03 of the Administrative Code. The service is intended for individuals with a mental health diagnosis resulting in functional deficits who need intensive community support to maintain stability and achieve recovery goals. It is intended to restore, rehabilitate, and support activities and routines of daily functioning within the individual's natural community environment by addressing impairments and barriers arising from and impacting the individual's mental illness. The service may be delivered at a fixed site, an individual's home, or in the community; the place of services delivery is to be based on the needs of the particular individual served.

(C)  For a provider to be certified to provide PSR service, the provider is to demonstrate all of the following:

(1)  That it is able to provide all of the following activities of the service:

(a)  Ongoing assessment of needs The provider is able to complete regular assessments of an individual's needs and barriers and, after each assessment, review the individual's comprehensive individualized treatment plan and document review results in accordance with rule 5122-27-03 of the Administrative Code.

(b)  Assistance in achieving personal independence in managing basic needs The provider is able to support an individual with independently managing their identified basic needs and life skills directly related to their mental illness, with progress measured by timeframes determined in the individual's comprehensive individualized treatment plan that reflect the individual's mental health-related functional goals.

(c)  Facilitation of further development of daily living skills The provider is able to facilitate development of an individual's identified daily living skills, directly related to the individual's mental illness, through structured practice and coaching until the individual meets the comprehensive individualized treatment plan goals that reflect their mental health-related functional needs.

(d)  Coordination of the comprehensive individualized treatment plan The provider is able to coordinate implementation of an individual's comprehensive individualized treatment plan by doing all of the following:

(i)  Ensuring that services identified in the plan are initiated and maintained by completing collateral contact and documenting the individual's engagement and progress;

(ii)  Assisting with the identification of and engagement with an individual's natural supports, such as family and peers; and

(iii)  Establishing linkages to formal community services/systems through collateral contact identified in an individual's comprehensive individualized treatment plan.

(e)  Symptom monitoring The provider is able to monitor the individual's psychiatric and/or substance use symptoms, review trends, and adjust coping strategies and comprehensive individualized treatment plan interventions to achieve stabilization within the community.

(f)  Coordination and/or assistance in crisis management and stabilization as needed Through collateral contact and direct service, the provider is able to coordinate crisis management and stabilization of an individual by implementing a safety plan, contacting crisis resources, and conducting post-crisis follow-up with the individual to reduce crisis frequency and severity and maintain community safety.

(g)  Education and training The provider is able to deliver tailored psychoeducation and skills training on identified topics and verify learning through demonstrated skills and real- world application linked to comprehensive individualized treatment plan goals.

(h)  Mental health interventions The provider is able to deliver targeted mental health interventions to reduce symptoms, behaviors, and thought patterns that interfere with achieving comprehensive individualized treatment goals. Intervention progress may be tracked by attendance, retention, symptom reduction, and/or functional gains.

(i)  Positive impact activities The provider is able to use positive impact and community functioning interventions such as, but not limited to, individualized education, modeling, role-play, community-based practice, problem-solving coaching, and prompting to help the individual recognize how their mental illness affects their community functioning, select and implement appropriate settings, initiate pro-social actions, and evaluate the outcome of each intervention as identified in the comprehensive individualized treatment plan.

(2)  That it is able to deliver the service to groups or individuals;

(3)  That it is able to deliver the service face-to-face, by telephone, or by video conferencing;

(4)  That regarding staffing, it meets all of the following criteria:

(a)  Subject to paragraph (D) of this rule, has an interdisciplinary team of staff qualified according to rule 5122-29-30 of the Administrative Code to provide and supervise the service;

(b)  Ensures that each particular staff member on the interdisciplinary team provides only those components of the service for which the individual possesses the appropriate qualifications or credential;

(c)  Ensures that when an individual served is enrolled in residential treatment or residential support facility setting, the service is provided by staff who are organized, distinct, and separate from the residential service as evidenced by staff job descriptions, time allocations or schedules, and development of service rates; and

(d)  Ensures that each individual to be served is assigned one staff member, based on the specific needs of the individual, who is the designated person to provide case coordination.

(5)  That it has a staff development plan that is based on identified individual needs of staff providing the PSR service. Evidence that the plan is being followed is to be maintained. The plan is to address, at a minimum, all of the following:

(a)  An understanding of systems of care, such as natural support systems, entitlements and benefits, inter-agency and intra-agency systems of care, crisis response systems and their purpose, and the intent and activities of the service;

(b)  Characteristics of the population to be served, such as psychiatric symptoms, medications, culture, and age/gender development; and

(c)  Knowledge of the service's purpose, intent, and activities.

(D)  A practitioner who possesses a high school diploma and specific training related to persons with mental health conditions or needs may perform the activities of the PSR service specified in paragraphs (C)(1)(b), (C)(1)(c), and (C)(1)(i) of this rule.

(E)  While, pursuant to paragraph (C)(1) of this rule, a provider is to demonstrate the ability to provide all activities of the PSR service to be certified for the service, a provider may contract with another provider certified under this rule to provide one or more activities of the service specified in that paragraph.
