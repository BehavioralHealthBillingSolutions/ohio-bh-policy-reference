# 5122-29-18 — Therapeutic behavioral services

## Rule Text

(A)  As used in this rule,

(1)  "Provider" means a provider described in rule 5122-25-01 of the Administrative Code to whom Chapter 5122-25 of the Administrative Code applies.

(2)  "Collateral contact" occurs when the provider contacts individuals who play a significant role in the service recipient's life.

(B)  Therapeutic behavioral services are intensive, one-on-one or group outpatient services for adults with a mental health diagnosis. They are supports and solution-focused interventions, delivered on a short-term basis, intended to achieve identified goals or objectives in an individual's comprehensive individualized treatment plan developed under rule 5122-27-03 of the Administrative Code and focused on reducing high-risk, disruptive, and/or self-destructive behaviors by building specific behavioral and emotional management skills. The service may be delivered in all settings that meet the needs of the individual.

(C)  For a provider to be certified to provide therapeutic behavioral services, the provider is to demonstrate all of the following:

(1)  That it is able to provide all of the following activities of therapeutic behavioral services:

(a)  Collateral contact The provider is able to do both of the following:

(i)  Consult with one or more eligible providers of the services, as specified in paragraph (C)(3) of this rule, for the purpose of addressing an individual's identified needs and participating in service planning. Such consultation may include planning for individualized supports and coordinating healthcare, behavioral healthcare, and non healthcare services in accordance with the individual's comprehensive individualized treatment plan; and

(ii)  Refer an individual to other healthcare, behavioral healthcare and non- healthcare services to avoid more restrictive levels of treatment.

(b)  Use of evidence-based techniques The provider is able to provide solution-focused interventions and emotional and behavioral management for an individual, drawn from evidence-based modalities to ameliorate targeted symptoms and improve the individual's capacity to cope with or prevent symptom manifestation.

(c)  Treatment planning With the individual's participation and consent, the provider is able to utilize strengths-based treatment planning including assisting the individual or individual's support systems (e.g., a parent, guardian, or other treatment professionals involved with the individual's care) in identifying needs and treatment goals associated with the individual's mental illness or behavioral health condition.

(d)  Restoration of self-management skills social skills and daily functioning The provider is able to provide therapeutic interventions designed to promote safe and adaptive alternatives to high-risk, disruptive, and/or self-destructive behaviors resulting in improved emotional and behavioral self-regulation and functioning; improved insight and self-awareness; increased community tenure; and effective functioning to support the individual's ability to maintain safety within the individual's natural environment as related to their diagnosed mental illness or behavioral health condition.

(e)  Crisis prevention and amelioration The provider is able to provide therapeutic support to assist an individual and the individual's support systems to identify potential psychiatric or personal crises, develop crisis management plans, and, as appropriate, provide collateral contact to restore stability and functioning to avoid more restrictive levels of treatment.

(2)  That for each individual to receive the services, it is able to monitor reductions or increases in symptom severity, the need for transition to a higher or lower level of care, and increases or decreases in social skills and daily functioning. Monitoring is needed to detect early signs of clinical deterioration, initiate timely intervention, evaluate response to treatment, adjust the comprehensive individualized treatment plan, and support the individual's recovery goals by strengthening coping skills, community participation, and independence in daily living.

(3)  That it employs or contracts with either or both of the following to provide the services:

(a)  One or more behavioral health practitioners who possess, at a minimum, a bachelor's or master's degree in social work, psychology, nursing, or in a related human services discipline; or

(b)  One or more qualified behavioral health specialists, as defined in rule 5122-29- 30 of the Administrative Code.
