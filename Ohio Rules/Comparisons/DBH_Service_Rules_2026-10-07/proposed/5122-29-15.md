# 5122-29-15 — Peer support services

## Rule Text

(A)  Peer support services are services for (1) individuals with a mental illness or substance us disorder, or both, who may also have a co-occurring developmental or intellectual disability and (2) the caregivers and families of such individuals.

(B)  Peer support services consist of activities that promote resiliency and recovery, self- determination, advocacy, well-being, and skill development. Peer support services are individualized, resiliency and recovery focused, non-clinical, and based on increasing knowledge and skills through a peer relationship that supports an individual's or family's ability to address needs, navigate systems and promote recovery, resiliency, and wellness. They promote family driven, youth guided, trauma informed care and cultural humility, encourage partnership with individuals and families, and advocate for informed choice.

(C)  As used in this rule:

(1)  "Peer support services provider" means a provider, described in rule 5122-25-01 of the Administrative Code to whom Chapter 5122-25 of the Administrative Code applies, that is certified under this rule to provide peer support services.

(2)  "Recovery" means the personal process of change in which an individual strives to improve their health and wellness, resiliency, and reach their full potential through self-directed actions.

(3)  "Resiliency" means the ability to recover from setbacks, adapt well to change, and keep going in the face of adversity. It is the process of adapting well in the face of adversity, trauma, tragedy, threats, or significant sources of stress such as family and relationship problems, serious health problems, or workplace and financial stressors.

(4)  "Wellness " means a broad approach for things individuals can do at their own pace, in their own time, and within their own abilities, that can help them feel better and live longer.

(D)  Peer support services may include, but are not limited to:

(1)  Ongoing exploration of recovery, resiliency, and wellness needs;

(2)  Supporting individuals and their caregivers and families in achieving goals through increased knowledge, skills and connection as identified by the individual or family;

(3)  Encouraging hope, empowerment, and personal agency in the recovery process;

(4)  Supporting the development of life skills and practical daily functioning;

(5)  Developing and working toward individualized recovery, resiliency, and wellness goals, while supporting multiple pathways;

(6)  Modeling personal responsibility for resiliency, recovery, and lived experience to teach, mentor, and inspire recovery and wellness;

(7)  Teaching and coaching skills to navigate behavioral health, social service, and community systems to effectively and efficiently utilize services;

(8)  Assisting individuals, caregivers, and families in addressing and eliminating barriers to achieve and maintain recovery;

(9)  Assisting with accessing, developing, and engaging natural supports, community resources, and formal or informal support systems;

(10)  Promoting coordination and linkage among providers, systems, and community resources;

(11)  Coordinating or assisting in crisis interventions and stabilization;

(12)  Conducting outreach and community education to increase awareness and engagement;

(13)  Attending and participating in team decision making or treatment team meetings;

(14)  Planning, developing, and facilitating peer recovery group or peer-led recovery activities;

(15)  Creating and updating recovery plans;

(16)  Providing mentoring, mutual support, and guidance based on lived experience;

(17)  Assisting individuals to engage in behavioral health services and overcome barriers to participation;

(18)  Engaging additional supports that promote active participation in the individual's recovery; and

(19)  Assisting individuals, caregivers, or families in the development of empowerment skills through advocacy and activities that mitigate discrimination and inspire hope.

(E)  Providing services in a culturally inclusive and competent manner which includes not practicing, condoning, facilitating, or collaborating in any form of discrimination on the basis of ethnicity, race, gender, sexual orientation, age, religion, national origin, marital status, political belief, or mental or physical disability.

(F)  Peer support services are not site specific but are to be provided in locations that meet the needs of the individual, caregiver, or families.

(G)  Peer support services may be facilitated to individuals, families, or groups.

(H)  Peer support services are to be provided by a person certified in accordance with in rule 5122-29-15.1 of the Administrative Code.

(I)  Peer support services providers are to report to the department of behavioral health any events involving a certified peer supporter (whether employed or volunteering) that would disqualify that certified peer supporter under rule 5122-29-15.1 of the Administrative Code.
