# 5122-29-12 — Driver intervention program

Source: supplied Service-Rules.pdf, October 7, 2026 PDF creation date.

Status: Draft – Not for Filing; no effective date stated.

## Rule Text

(A)  A driver intervention program is a program of screening, education, and referral for individuals who are arrested or convicted of operation of a vehicle or water craft under the influence of alcohol or a drug of abuse under section 4511.19 or 1547.11 of the Revised Code or a substantially similar municipal ordinance or other alcohol-related traffic statute or ordinance.

(B)  No entity may operate, or purport to operate, a driver intervention program in Ohio unless it has received driver intervention program certification from the department.

(C)  Except as otherwise provided in this rule, the provisions of this rule apply to all driver intervention programs in Ohio, public or private. The provisions in this rule do not negate the necessity of driver intervention programs to be certified providers in accordance with the provisions of Chapter 5122-25 of the Administrative Code.

(D)  A driver intervention program is to provide to the department all of the following at the time of seeking initial certification under rule 5122-25-02 of the Administrative Code, renewal certification under rule 5122-25-04 of the Administrative Code, or when seeking to update its certification of certifiable services or supports under rule 5122-25-03 of the Administrative Code:

(1)  The total number of hours of operation, including the total number of program hours;

(2)  If services are provided at a resident camp, a copy of the camp permit issued by the appropriate board of health pursuant to rule 3701-25-02 of the Administrative Code;

(3)  If services are provided at a hotel or motel, a copy of the facility's occupancy and use certificate and annual fire inspection report or, if those are not available, a copy of the facility's hotel or motel license issued by the division of state fire marshal in the Ohio department of commerce.

(E)  Each driver intervention program is to have either representatives from law enforcement officers, judges, prosecuting and defense attorneys, peer service organizations, social service organizations, and treatment centers on its governing authority or establish an advisory board with such representatives. If an advisory board is established, both of the following are to be the case:

(1)  The board is to meet annually; and

(2)  Minutes are to be taken and maintained.

(F)  Program administration:

(1)  Each driver intervention program is to have a program director who is responsible for the overall day-to-day operation of the driver intervention program. The driver intervention program director is responsible to the governing authority of the organization. If the driver intervention program is a component of a larger organization, the driver intervention program director may report to the executive director/chief executive officer of the organization, who would then report to the governing authority. A program director is to meet the following qualifications:

(a)  Have a bachelor's degree and two years' experience in alcohol and other drug addiction services or an allied profession to include one year as a supervisor;

(b)  Have three years' experience in alcohol and other drug addiction services or an allied profession to include a minimum of one year as a supervisor; or

(c)  Have three years of experience in business administration to include a minimum of one year as a supervisor.

(2)  The position description of the driver intervention program director is to include, at a minimum, the following responsibilities;

(a)  Overseeing the day-to-day operations of the driver intervention program;

(b)  Developing and implementing the policies and procedures of the driver intervention program;

(c)  Developing and revising as necessary, the driver intervention program's education curriculum;

(d)  Preparing an annual plan for the operation of the driver intervention program;

(e)  Implementing the driver intervention program's quality assurance and improvement activities and findings;

(f)  Hiring and terminating driver intervention program staff; and

(g)  Ensuring that the driver intervention program is operating in accordance with the department's driver intervention program certification standards.

(G)  Each driver intervention program is to maintain both of the following:

(1)  An outline of the current education curriculum of the driver intervention program.

(2)  A copy of the revenue and expenditure budget for the driver intervention program.

(H)  Each driver intervention program is to meet, at a minimum, all of the following accessibility requirements for persons with physical disabilities:

(1)  Entrances, hallways and spaces where services are provided, as well as office space for employees are to be accessibile to persons with physical disabilities.

(2)  The facility is to have at least one bathroom accessible to persons with physical disabilities.

(3)  The facility is to have designated parking space(s) for persons with physical disabilities based on the Americans with disabilities act accessibility guidelines.

(4)  The facility is to have at least one drinking fountain accessible to persons with physical disabilities.

(5)  Facility is to have at least one telephone that is accessible to persons with physical disabilities.

(6)  Each residential driver intervention program is to have at least one shower facility accessible to persons with physical disabilities.

(I)  Emergency medical plan and first aid supplies

(1)  Each driver intervention program is to have a written emergency medical plan that includes, at a minimum, all of the following:

(a)  The current emergency telephone numbers for fire, emergency squad, police and poison control;

(b)  The location of first aid supplies at the program site during operation of the driver intervention program;

(c)  General instructions for medical emergencies including supervision of clients during the emergency;

(d)  General instructions in case of illness of a client; and

(e)  A procedure for documenting unusual incidents and notifying families.

(2)  A copy of the emergency medical plan is to be conspicuously posted at the program site during the operation of each driver intervention program.

(J)  Client records

(1)  Each driver intervention program is to have written policies and/or procedures for maintaining a uniform client records system that complies with rule 5122-27-02 of the Administrative Code and includes, at a minimum, all of the following:

(a)  A statement that program staff, contract employees, volunteers, and student interns are not to convey to a person outside of the program that an individual attends or receives services from the driver intervention program, or disclose any information identifying a client as an alcohol or other drug services client unless the client consents in writing for the release of information; the disclosure is allowed by a court order; the disclosure is to entities with which the provider has entered into a qualified service organization agreement

(QSOA) pursuant to 42 C.F.R. part 2; or the disclosure is made to qualified personnel for a medical emergency, research, audit or program evaluation purposes. The driver intervention program has the authority to deny services if a client refuses consent to the release of information.

(b)  A statement that the federal laws and regulations do not protect any threat to commit or any information about a crime committed by a client either at the program or against any individual who works for the driver intervention program.

(c)  A statement that the federal laws and regulations do not protect any information about suspected child abuse or neglect from being reported under state law to appropriate state or local authorities.

(d)  A statement that disclosure made with the client's written consent is to be consistent with 42 C.F.R. part 2 and include one of the written statements mandated by 42 C.F.R. 2.32.

(e)  A policy on the access of client records by clients, staff, and others.

(f)  Components of client records and timelines, when applicable, for completing each component.

(g)  A policy on the storage of client records that requires records be maintained in accordance with 42 C.F.R. part 2.

(h)  A policy on the destruction of client records to include the requirement that records be maintained for at least six years after clients have been discharged from the program. Client records are to be destroyed to maintain client confidentiality as required by state and federal law.

(2)  A record is to be maintained for each client of a driver intervention program. Each record is to include, at a minimum, all of the following components:

(a)  Identification of client (name of client and/or client identification number);

(b)  Client fee agreement;

(c)  Consent for services;

(d)  Documentation reflecting receipt of the schedule for the driver intervention program being attended by the client;

(e)  Documentation reflecting receipt of the driver intervention program rules and/or expectations of clients;

(f)  Documentation reflecting receipt of the program's policy on client rights that lists the client rights required by this rule;

(g)  Documentation reflecting receipt of the program's client grievance procedure;

(h)  Documentation reflecting receipt of a written summary of the federal laws and regulations that indicate the confidentiality of client records are protected as required by 42 C.F.R. part 2;

(i)  Intake report;

(j)  Identification of at least two screening instruments that were administered to the client and documentation of the results of both tests;

(k)  Results and recommendations of the screening;

(l)  Recommendations for alcohol and/or drug;

(m)  Assessment, if completed by the driver intervention program;

(n)  Any recommendations made to a court or other organization;

(o)  Date of each group session provided;

(p)  Length of each group session provided;

(q)  Topic/content of each group session provided;

(r)  Client's response/feedback during each group session; and

(s)  Disclosure of client information forms, when applicable.

(3)  Disclosure of client information forms is to include all of the following information as required by 42 C.F.R. part 2:

(a)  Name of program making the disclosure;

(b)  Name or title of the individual or the name of the organization to which the disclosure is to be made;

(c)  Name of the client;

(d)  Purpose of the disclosure;

(e)  Type and amount of information to be disclosed;

(f)  Original signature of the client or person autorized to give consent;

(g)  Date client or authorized person signed the form;

(h)  Statement that the consent is subject to revocation at any time except to the extent the program or person who is to make the disclosure has already acted in reliance on it; and

(i)  The date, event, or condition upon which the consent will expire, unless revoked before that specified time.

(4)  Each client record is to include a program completion report that that is to include, at a minimum, all of the following documentation:

(a)  Results and recommendations of the screening.

(b)  Any recommendations for alcohol and/or drug assessment.

(c)  Any recommendations made to a court or other organization.

(d)  Number of hours of driver intervention programming the client attended.

(e)  Any referrals made to alcohol and drug addiction treatment programs and any referrals made to other organizations.

(f)  Date, signature, and credentials of the program director, services supervisor or program staff of the driver intervention program who makes the recommendation.

(g)  Summary of client's participation.

(h)  ) Disclosure of client information form that is prepared in accordance with 42 C.F.R. part 2 for information released to courts, organizations and/or individuals and for management information reports to the department. A copy of the report is to be sent to the court or organization that referred the client to the driver intervention program.

(5)  A policy and procedure, in accordance with 42 C.F.R. part 2, for tracking clients for a reasonable time following program completion.

(6)  If a program maintains electronic client records, the program is to be able to produce hard copies of client records upon legally valid requests and have a written policy and procedure indicating how client original signatures and staff original signatures are obtained and verified for documentation.

(7)  If a program discontinues operations or is taken over or acquired by another entity, it is to comply with 42 C.F.R. 2.19, which governs the disposition of records by discontinued programs.

(8)  Providers are to maintain treatment records for at least seven years after a client has been discharged from a program or services are no longer provided.

(K)  Intake report

(1)  An intake report is to be completed for each client at the beginning of the first day of the driver intervention program. Documentation is to include, at a minimum, all of the following:

(a)  Client identification number and name;

(b)  Prescription and over-the-counter drugs being taken by the client;

(c)  Type and amount of any medications brought to the program;

(d)  Special dietary requirements;

(e)  Known allergies, including but not limited to food and drug reactions;

(f)  Pregnancy status;

(g)  Special needs; and

(h)  Name, address, and telephone number of a person who is to be contacted in the event of an emergency.

(2)  Baggage and materials brought to the driver intervention program are to be inspected to ascertain that they do not contain contraband, which includes, at a minimum, illegal drugs, alcohol, or firearms. Documentation is to appear in the intake report.

(3)  The intake report is to be dated and signed by the staff member completing the intake report.

(L)  Screening

(1)  Screening means a preliminary gathering and sorting of information used to determine whether a comprehensive assessment is appropriate.

(2)  Each client of a driver intervention program is to be administered at least two evidence-informed screening instruments. Those results are to be recorded in the client's record.

(3)  An individual screening interview is to be done with each client to discuss the screening results, recommendations, and referrals made to a referring court or other organization. Screening interviews are to include, at a minimum, all of the following:

(a)  Client identification;

(b)  Presenting problem and/or precipitating factors leading to the need for screening;

(c)  Past and present use of alcohol and other drugs;

(d)  History of treatment for alcohol and other drug abuse;

(e)  Medical problems;

(f)  Legal history;

(g)  Recommendations for referral, if applicable, for a comprehensive assessment to determine the extent and severity of alcohol and other drug abuse problems and need for treatment; and

(h)  Date, signature, and credentials of program staff who completed the screening.

(M)  Referral for assessment

(1)  Each driver intervention program is to have a written procedure for making referrals for assessment which requires that a completed release of information be obtained prior to contacting a program.

(2)  Each driver intervention program is to have a written policy stating that the basis for making a recommendation to a court or other organization for alcohol and drug addiction comprehensive assessment will include, at a minimum, both of the following:

(a)  Results of the two screening instruments and screening interview described in paragraph (L) of this rule; and

(b)  Observations of the client during screening, client education on alcohol and drug abuse and addiction, and group sessions.

(3)  Each driver intervention program is to have a written policy for submitting a copy of the report to the court or organization that referred the client to the driver intervention program, as requested by the referral source.

(N)  A driver intervention program may conduct client assessments. Assessments are to be conducted in accordance with rule 5122-29-03 of the Administrative Code.

(O)  Dietary services: Each driver intervention program that prepares and/or serves meals as part of its daily scheduled activities is to operate its dietary services in accordance with laws, regulations, or ordinances of the state medical board of Ohio, Ohio department of health, and/or the appropriate local health department.

(P)  Pharmaceutical services A driver intervention program is subject to rule 5122-26-15 of the Administrative Code regarding the purchase, receipt, storage, distribution, return, and destruction of medication. Clients are not to be denied driver intervention services solely due to their use of prescribed psychotropic medications.

(Q)  Small group sessions A driver intervention program is to include small group sessions. Small group sessions are not to exceed a staff to client ratio of one to fifteen and the total number of clients in a group session is not to exceed fifteen, regardless of the number of staff.

(R)  Active supervision and monitoring of clients A driver intervention program is to have at least one staff member who is on-site and actively supervising and/or monitoring clients at all times during the program.

(S)  Program completion reports A driver intervention program is to ensure that a program completion report is prepared for each client.

(T)  Non-residential driver intervention programs:

(1)  Each non-residential driver intervention program is to consist of at least thirteen hours of alcohol and drug addiction programming that includes, at a minimum, all of the following:

(a)  One hour of screening and individual contact;

(b)  Eight hours of client education on alcohol and drug abuse and addiction including traffic safety education; and

(c)  Four hours of small group discussion sessions.

(2)  Each non-residential driver intervention program is not to deliver more than eight hours of alcohol and drug addiction programming to clients each day.

(3)  If a non-residential driver intervention program is operated within a residential driver intervention program, its programmatic content is to be the same as that of the residential driver intervention program and include the mid-day and evening meals.

(4)  If a non-residential driver intervention program operates its program for five hours or more on any day, the program is to have provisions for a mid-day meal of at least thirty minutes. This mid-day meal is to be included in the program's schedule that is available to clients upon request. Time for meals is not to supplant any of the minimum thirteen-hour alcohol and drug programming.

(U)  Forty-eight hour residential driver intervention programs Each forty-eight hour residential driver intervention program is to consist of at least sixteen hours of alcohol and drug addiction programming that includes, at a minimum, all of the following:

(1)  One hour of screening and individual contact;

(2)  Ten hours of client education on alcohol and drug abuse and addiction including traffic safety education; and

(3)  Five hours of small group discussion sessions.

(V)  Seventy-two hour residential driver intervention programs

(1)  Each seventy-two hour residential driver intervention program is to consist of at least twenty-one hours of alcohol and drug addiction programming that includes, at a minimum, all of the following:

(a)  One hour of screening and individual;

(b)  Fifteen hours of client education on alcohol and drug abuse and addiction including traffic safety education; and

(c)  Five hours of small group discussion sessions.

(2)  The program is to provide morning, mid-day, and evening meals of at least thirty minutes duration.

(W)  Each driver intervention program is to have a written educational curriculum that includes, at a minimum, all of the following:

(1)  Traffic safety education as it relates to alcohol and drug use;

(2)  Client education on alcohol and drug abuse and addiction; and

(3)  Small group discussion topics.

(X)  Each program is to prepare an educational curriculum for its driver intervention. That program is to be approved by the program's governing authority and will include, at a minimum, all of the following:

(1)  A timetable for conducting the program;

(2)  An instructional outline for each topic/module; and

(3)  A method of instuction, including audio/visual aids.

(Y)  Each program is to have a program schedule that can be given to clients upon request.

(Z)  Traffic safety education:

(1)  Traffic safety education is to include, at a minimum, all of the following information on the criminal justice system and relevant law:

(a)  Blood alcohol content (BAC), drugs, and impairment;

(b)  Levels of license suspension and revocation; and

(c)  Fines and levels of incarceration.

(2)  The driving task and the specific psychomotor skills required.

(3)  The physiological and psychological effects of alcohol and other drugs on driving performance.

(4)  Traffic safety education sessions are not to exceed an instructor to client ratio of one to forty eight.

(AA)  Client education on alcohol and drug abuse and addiction

(1)  Client education on alcohol and drug abuse and addiction is to include, at a minimum, all of the following:

(a)  Physical and psychological aspects of the use of alcohol and other drugs;

(b)  Combining the use of alcohol with other drugs;

(c)  Social consequences of the use of alcohol and other drugs;

(d)  Signs and symptoms of abuse and dependence of alcohol and other drugs;

(e)  Dysfunctional behavior resulting from the use of alcohol and other drugs;

(f)  Progressive nature of alcohol and drug abuse and dependence;

(g)  Abstinence as a lifestyle and self-help programs such as alcoholics anonymous and narcotics anonymous; and

(h)  Treatment alternatives and local resources.

(2)  Programming does not include the following:

(a)  Individual and/or group counseling;

(b)  Self-help study; or

(c)  Anger management and stress reduction therapies.

(3)  Staffing for client education group sessions on alcohol and drug abuse and addiction are not to exceed an instructor to client ratio of one to forty eight.

(BB)  Indigent clients

(1)  A driver intervention program that receives funds that originate and/or pass through the department is to have a policy and procedure that states that admission to the driver intervention program will not be denied because an individual is indigent as long as public funds are available to cover the cost of the program.

(2)  Indigent individuals are persons who have in their possession at the beginning of a driver intervention program a document indicating the client is a recipient of public assistance, including, but not limited to, supplemental security income, social security disability income, medicaid, Ohio works first, or another category of assistance as defined by the department.

(3)  The client record of indigent individuals is to include a photocopy of the documentation that was used to verify indigent status.
