The Common Sense Initiative is established in R.C. 107.61 to eliminate excessive and
duplicative rules and regulations that stand in the way of job creation. Under the Common
Sense Initiative, agencies must balance the critical objectives of regulations that have an
adverse impact on business with the costs of compliance by the regulated parties. Agencies
should promote transparency, responsiveness, predictability, and flexibility while developing
regulations that are fair and easy to follow. Agencies should prioritize compliance over
punishment, and to that end, should utilize plain language in the development of regulations.

10. What input was provided by the stakeholders, and how did that input affect the draft
    regulation being proposed by the Agency?
     Themes of stakeholder comments:

        1. Clarification and alignment of definitions: adding or aligning the definition of substance
           use disorder across rules, clarifying terms such as “co-occurring conditions,” “collateral
           contact,” and “self-management skills.” Changes were made to ensure consistency and
           reduce ambiguity across behavioral health rules.
        2. Restoration of existing or prior language for accuracy: restoring references to resilience,
           recovery, and wellness in peer support rules, re-including assessment, referral, monitoring
           and follow-up in SUD case management. Maintain continuity with existing regulatory
           frameworks and avoid unintended narrowing of services.
        3. Removal of ambiguous, duplicative, or confusing language: removing references to
           MRSS/adult crisis services where necessary, eliminating terms like “skilled treatment,”
           which are not standard. Streamline rules to enhance clarity and operation usability.
        4. Updating service requirements to match practice and policy needs: conditioning prescriber
           availability on whether a program offers medication management, allowing SUD case
           management to occur in group settings, updating expectations for peer-run organizations,
           including hours of operation and funding clarity. Modernize service standards and reduce
           unnecessary regulatory burden.
        5. Enhancing cross-rule and cross-agency consistency: adding references to additional
           background checks guided by DCY rules, clarifying citation issues and cross-rule
           references. Promote uniformity across interconnected behavioral health systems.
        6. Broadening access and eligibility where appropriate: clarifying that individuals with co-
           occurring or secondary conditions remain eligible for ACT. Reduces unnecessary
           eligibility limitations and ensure inclusive service access.
