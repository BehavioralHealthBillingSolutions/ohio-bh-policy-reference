k. Provider Advisory Council 
 
The MCOP must establish a provider advisory council. 
 
The MCOP must hold provider advisory council meetings no less than on a semi-annual 
basis. The MCOP must offer meeting attendance in person, by phone, or by webinar. 
 
The MCOP must ensure that the provider advisory council is composed of a wide array of 
provider types. 
 
The purpose of the provider advisory council is for the MCOP to gather input, discuss and 
learn about issues affecting providers, identify challenges and barriers, problem-solve, share 
information, and collectively find ways to improve and strengthen the health care service 
delivery system. 
 
The MCOP's provider advisory council must be chaired by the MCOP's Administrator/Chief 
Executive Officer (CEO)/Chief Operating Officer (COO) or designee. 
 
The MCOP must invite ODM to attend provider advisory council meetings and provide an 
agenda to ODM in advance of the meetings. 
 
The MCOP must report on its provider advisory council activities (Provider Advisory Council 
Activity Report) as specified in Appendix P, Chart of Deliverables, including meeting dates, 
provider advisory council attendees, provider advisory council recommendations, and MCOP 
responses or follow-up activities to provider advisory council recommendations.

7. Call Center Requirements 
a. General  
 
The MCOP must ensure access to medical advice, behavioral health crisis, care management 
support, and prior authorization, coverage determination and appeals services through 
centralized, toll-free 24 hour, seven days a week (24/7) call-in systems available nationwide.  
 
The MCOP must ensure its call center systems have the capabilities to meet all performance 
and reporting requirements as specified in this Agreement.  
 
The MCOP must maintain a log for the 24/7 call center systems that include at a minimum 
the: 
 Identification of the caller and the member; 
 Date and time of call; 
 Reason for and disposition of the call; 
 PCP or another provider relevant to the call; and 
 Name and title of person taking the call. 
 
The MCOP's call center systems must have services or capabilities available to assist 
individuals with communication needs and disabilities, including individuals with hearing 
impairments and individuals with limited English proficiency.  
 
The MCOP must ensure that call center staff are knowledgeable about the MyCare Ohio 
program and have appropriate access to information pertaining to MyCare Ohio 
membership (e.g., membership status, covered benefits, provider network, person-centered 
care plans, etc.).  
 
The MCOP must implement procedures to ensure emergent issues are identified and 
assigned the highest priority. 
 
The MCOP must meet the call center standards as identified below. If access to call center 
systems is facilitated through a single call center service line with auto-prompts to transfer 
the caller, the MCOP must have a process to measure call center standards from the time of 
selecting the auto prompt. If the MCOP uses the member services line to answer all call 
center contacts (i.e., the MCOP does not offer an auto prompt to transfer), then call center 
standards for member services apply. ODM will inform the MCOP of any changes to call 
center standards. 
 
The MCOP must report the MCOP's performance in meeting the call center standards 
identified below for its medical advice line; behavioral health crisis line; care management 
support services line; prior authorization, coverage determinations, and appeals call center 
line; member services line; provider services line; and pharmacy technical health call center 
line by submitting a MyCare Call Center Report to ODM as specified in Appendix P, Chart of 
Deliverables. The MCOP must report call center performance standards more frequently if 
required by ODM.

contact their PCP or any other entity prior to contacting the 24/7 call center systems for

one auto-prompt to get the caller to a live contact.

 The MCOP must staff its medical advice line with appropriately trained medical

medical professionals" as physicians, physician assistants, licensed practical nurses,

(URAC) health call center standards for call center abandonment rate, blockage rate,

The MCOP must ensure that its 24/7 call-in systems for prior authorization, coverage

 Indicates that the mailbox is secure;

the member or provider is making an expedited or standard request);

twenty-four (24) hours of calls for expedited and standard requests; and

expedited appeal requests and seven calendar days for standard appeal requests.

Member Services Call Center Performance Standards 
 The MCOP must meet or exceed the following call center standards for its member 
services call center: 
 90% of calls answered within 30 seconds after the interactive voice 
response (IVR) system, touch-tone response system, or recorded greeting 
interaction, before reaching a live person; 
 Disconnect rate not to exceed 5%. The disconnect rate is defined as the 
number of calls unexpectedly dropped divided by the total number of calls 
made to the member services call center; 
 Hold time not to exceed 60 seconds. Hold time is defined as the time a 
caller is placed on hold by a live call center representative;  
 Availability of interpreters for 80% of incoming calls requiring an interpreter 
within eight minutes of reaching the MSR at no cost to the caller; 
 Connection of 80% of incoming calls requiring TTY services to a TTY operator 
within seven minutes; 
 
All inquiries that require a call back returned within one business day of 
receipt; and 
 A minimum 70% of all calls resolved during the first call. 
 The MCOP must have a process to measure the time from when the call is answered 
to the point at which a member reaches a MSR capable of responding to the 
member's question in a manner that is sensitive to the member's language and 
cultural needs. 
 The MCOP must comply with any changes or updates to URAC call center standards
