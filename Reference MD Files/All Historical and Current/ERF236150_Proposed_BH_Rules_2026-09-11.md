## 5160-8-05 - Behavioral health services - other licensed professionals



*Source: ERF 236150, PDF pages 3-11.*



Behavioral health services-other licensed professionals.

(A) Scope. This rule sets forth provisions governing payment for behavioral health services

provided by certain licensed professionals in non-institutional settings.

(1) Provisions governing payment for behavioral health services as the following service types are set forth in the indicated part of the Administrative Code:

(a) Cost-based clinic services, Chapter 5160-28; and

(b) Medicaid school program services, Chapter 5160-35.

(2) For services provided in a nursing facility, the cost for behavioral health services are paid directly to the provider of services and not through the nursing facility

per diem rate.

(B) Definitions for the purposes of this rule.

(1) "Behavioral health service" is a service or procedure that is performed for the diagnosis and treatment of mental, behavioral, substance use, or emotional disorders by a licensed professional or under the supervision of a licensed professional. As it is used in this rule, the term includes neither services rendered by a psychiatrist

nor medication management.

(2) "Licensed psychologist" has the same meaning as in rule 4732-1-02 of the Administrative

Code.

(3) "Independent practitioner" is a term used in this rule to designate the following persons who hold a valid license to practice in accordance with the indicated portion of the Administrative Code:

(a) Licensed professional clinical counselor, rule 4757-13-03 ;

(b) Licensed independent social worker, rule 4757-19-02 ; (c) Independent marriage and family therapist, rule 4757-25-04 ;

(d) Licensed independent chemical dependency counselor, rule 4758-5-05 of the Administrative Code; and

(e) School psychologist licensed by the state board of psychology has the same meaning as in rule 4732-3-01 of the Administrative Code and who is engaged in the "practice of school psychology" as that term is defined

in section 4732.01 of the Revised Code.

(4) "Supervised practitioner" is a collective term used in this rule to designate the following persons who hold a valid license to practice under general supervision in accordance with the indicated portion of the Administrative Code:

(a) Licensed professional counselor, rule 4757-13-02 ;

(b) Licensed social worker, rule 4757-19-01 ; (c) Marriage and family therapist, rule 4757- 25-03 ;

(d) Licensed chemical dependency counselor II, rule 4758-5-03 ; and

(e) Licensed chemical dependency counselor III, rule 4758-5-04

.

(5) "Supervised trainee" is a collective term used in this rule to designate the following individuals :

(a) Registered counselor trainee, defined in rule 4757-1-10 of the Administrative Code;

(b) Registered social work trainee, defined in rule 4757-1-10 of the Administrative Code;

(c) Marriage and family therapist trainee, defined in rule 4757-1-10 of the Administrative Code;

(d) Chemical dependency counselor assistant, defined in rule 4757- 5-01 of the Administrative Code; and

(e) Any individual registered with the Ohio board of psychology in compliance with requirements in rule 4732-15-01 of the Administrative Code, working under the supervision of a licensed psychologist, and assigned by the supervising psychologist a title appearing in rule 4732- 13-03 of the Administrative Code, such as "assistant," "psychology assistant," "psychology intern," "psychology fellow," or "psychology resident."

(6) "General supervision" is defined as supervision is available, although not necessarily present in the practice setting, to provide assistance and direction throughout the provision of services. A supervisor who is not physically present has the capability to provide immediate consultation by communications device

and can travel to the practice setting quickly in case of emergency.

(7) "Direct supervision" is defined as supervision is present in the practice setting, although not necessarily in the same room, and is immediately available to provide assistance and direction throughout the provision of services. Neither availability by telephone nor presence nearby or outside the practice setting

constitutes direct supervision.

(8) "Independent practice" is a business arrangement in which a professional is not subject to the administrative and professional control of an employer such as an institution, physician, or agency. In particular, a professional working from an office that is located within an entity is considered to be in independent practice when both of the following conditions are met:

(a) The part of the entity constituting the office of the professional is used solely for that purpose and is separately identifiable from the rest of the facility; and

(b) The professional maintains a private practice (i.e., offers services to the general public as well as to the customers, residents, or patients of the

entity), and the practice is not owned, either in part or in total, by the entity.

(C) Provider requirements.

(1) A licensed psychologist or licensed independent practitioner must be enrolled in the medicaid program as an eligible provider, even if services are rendered

under the supervision of another eligible provider.

(2) A licensed psychologist in independent practice or independent practitioner in independent practice who can participate in the medicare program either must do so or, if the practice is limited to pediatric treatment, must meet all requirements for medicare participation other than serving medicare

beneficiaries.

(D) Coverage.

(1) Payment may be made for the following behavioral health services:

(a) Psychiatric diagnostic evaluation;

(b) Psychological and neuropsychological testing;

(c) Assessment and behavior change intervention:

(i) Alcohol or substance (other than tobacco) abuse, structured assessment and brief intervention, fifteen to thirty minutes;

(ii) Alcohol or substance (other than tobacco) abuse, structured assessment and intervention, greater than thirty minutes;

(iii) Effective July 1, 2027, child and adolescent needs and strengths (CANS). when administered by a CANS assessor.

(d) Therapeutic services:

(i) Individual psychotherapy:

(a) Psychotherapy, with patient and/or family member; (b) Psychotherapy for crisis ;

(ii) Family psychotherapy for which the primary purpose is the treatment of the patient and not family members:

(a) Family psychotherapy without patient present; and

(b) Family psychotherapy with patient present;

(iii) Group psychotherapy:

(a) Group psychotherapy; and

(b) Multiple-family group psychotherapy;

(iv) Interactive complexity

(2) Payment may be made to the following eligible providers for a behavioral health service rendered as indicated:

(a) To a physician, group practice, clinic, or a community behavioral health center that is certified by the Ohio department of behavioral health , for a behavioral health service rendered by a licensed psychologist, or independent practitioner, employed by or under contract with the physician group practice, clinic or community behavioral health center;

(b) To a physician group practice, clinic, a community behavioral health center that meets the requirements found in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code, physician, advanced practice registered nurse, physician assistant, licensed psychologist in independent practice or independent practitioner in independent practice for a behavioral health service rendered by a supervised practitioner or supervised trainee under general supervision of the supervising practitioner

as needed.

(c) To a physician group practice, clinic, a community behavioral health center that meets the requirements found in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code, physician, advanced practice registered nurse, physician assistant, licensed psychologist in independent practice or independent practitioner in independent practice for a behavioral health service rendered by a supervised trainee under direct supervision if the following conditions are met:

(i) The professional responsible for the patient's care has contact with the patient during the initial visit and contact not less often than once per quarter (or during each visit if visits are scheduled more than

three months apart).

(ii) The professional responsible for the patient's care reviews and updates the patient's medical record at least once after each

treatment visit.

(d) To a physician, advanced practice registered nurse, physician assistant, licensed psychologist in independent practice, or independent practitioner in independent practice for a behavioral health service personally rendered by that health care professional;

.

(e) To a federally qualified health center (FQHC) or rural health center (RHC)

in accordance with Chapter 5160-28 of the Administrative Code.

(3) At any time, with proper notice as defined in rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code, the Ohio department of medicaid (ODM) may subject the services listed in paragraph (D)(1) of this rule to limitations on initiation and continuation of services, frequency, scope, and duration of services and allowances for services to be provided on the same day. Current and historical limitations will be posted on the master list on the ODM website as described in rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code.

(E) Constraints.

(1) Every behavioral health service reported on a claim must be within the scope of practice of the licensed professional, with appropriate certification or training for the service, who renders or supervises it and must be performed in accordance with any supervision requirements established in law, regulation,

statute, or rule.

(2) No payment will be made under this rule for the following activities:

(a) Services that are rendered by an unlicensed individual other than a supervised trainee;

(b) Activities, testing, or diagnosis conducted for purposes specifically related to education;

(c) Services that are unrelated to the treatment of a specific behavioral health diagnosis but serve primarily to enhance skills or to provide general information, examples of which are given in the following non-exhaustive list:

(i) Encounter groups, workshops, marathon sessions, or retreats;

(ii) Sensitivity training;

(iii) Sexual competency training;

(iv) Recreational therapy (e.g., arts and crafts, game play, dance, listening to music watching television, movies or other forms of visual media, cosmetology, snack or meal breaks );

(v) Services intended primarily for social interaction, diversion, or sensory stimulation; and

(vi) The teaching or monitoring of activities of daily living (such as grooming and personal hygiene);

(d) Psychotherapy services if the patient cannot establish a relationship with the provider because of a cognitive deficit;

(e) Family therapy for the purpose of training family members or caregivers in the management of the patient; and

(f) Self-administered or self-scored tests of cognitive function.

(F) Documentation of services.

(1) The patient's medical record must substantiate the medical necessity of services performed, and each record is expected to bear the signature and indicate the

discipline of the professional who recorded it.

(a) All relevant diagnoses pertaining to medical or physical conditions as well as to behavioral health;

(b) A treatment plan which must be completed within five sessions or one month of admission, whichever is longer and must specify mutually agreed upon treatment goals, track responses to ongoing treatment, and present a prognosis that documents that the plan has been reviewed with the patient and, as appropriate, with family members, parents, legal guardians or

custodians or significant others;.

(c) The inability or refusal of the patient to participate in treatment planning or

services must be documented and the reason given.

(d) Test results, if applicable, with interpretation;

(e) Evidence that the patient has sufficient cognitive capacity to benefit from treatment; and

(f) Discharge summaries which include date of admission, date of last service, outcome of the service and recommendations and referrals made to the

patient.

(2) The following items must be included as progress note documentation and shall be completed at a minimum on a per provision basis, or on a daily or weekly basis:

(a) The type, description, date, time of day, duration, location and, if documenting weekly services, the frequency of treatment, with dates of service;

(b) A description of the patient's current symptoms and changes in functional impairment;

(c) Changes in medications taken by or prescribed for the patient when applicable;

(d) The amount of time spent by the provider with the patient;

(e) The amount of time spent by the provider in interpreting and reporting on procedures represented by "Central Nervous System Testing" codes, when applicable;

(f) Progress notes shall include assessment of the patient's progress or lack of progress and a brief description of the progress made, if any, significant changes in symptoms, functioning, or events in the life of the patient and recommendation for modifications to the treatment plan, if applicable; and

(g) Evidence of clinical supervision, as required.

(G) Claim payment. The payment amount for a behavioral health service stated in the appendix to rule 5160-27-03 of the Administrative Code and rendered by a community behavioral health center that meets the requirements found in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code is the lesser of the provider's submitted charge or the amount specified in the appendix to rule 5160-27-03 of the Administrative Code. For all other providers of behavioral health services, the payment amount is the lesser of the provider's submitted charge or the applicable percentage of the amount specified in the appendix to rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code:

(1) For psychological testing, one hundred per cent;

(2) For a behavioral health service other than psychological testing, the percentage differs according to the provider who rendered it:

(a) For a service rendered by a physician, an advanced practice registered nurse, a physician assistant, or a licensed psychologist, it is one hundred per cent of the maximum rate

.

(b) For a service rendered by a licensed practitioner or a supervised practitioner, it is eighty-five per cent of the maximum rate

.

(c) For a service rendered by a supervised trainee/assistant under direct

supervision, the rate of their supervising practitioner.

(d) For a service rendered by a supervised psychology assistant or trainee under general supervision, it is eighty-five per cent of the rate of

their supervising practitioner.

(e) For a service rendered by a supervised social worker trainee or assistant, marriage and family therapist trainee, chemical dependency counselor assistant, or counselor trainee under general supervision, it is 72.25 per

cent of the maximum fee.



## 5160-27-01 - Eligible provider of community behavioral health services



*Source: ERF 236150, PDF pages 12-14.*



Eligible provider of community behavioral health services.

(A) For the purposes of this chapter, an "eligible billing provider" is an entity that meets the conditions in paragraph (A)(1) or (A)(2) of this rule. An "eligible rendering provider" is an individual who meets one or more of the conditions stated in paragraphs (A)

(3) to (A)(8) of this rule and is employed by or under contract with an eligible billing

provider.

(1) An entity certified by the Ohio department of behavioral health operating in accordance with sections 5119.35 and 5119.36 of the Revised Code and Chapters 5122-24 to 5122-29 and Chapter 5160-1 of the Administrative Code and providing mental health or substance use disorder

treatment services.

(2) An out-of-state entity furnishing mental health and/or substance use disorder services and operating in accordance with rule 5160-1-11 of the Administrative

Code.

(3) Physician or physician assistant in accordance with agency 4731 and agency 4730 of the Administrative Code, respectively, and Chapter 5160-4 of the Administrative Code, or a clinical nurse specialist or certified nurse practitioner,

as described in Chapter 4723. of the Revised Code.

(4) Registered nurse or licensed practical nurse as defined in section 4723.01 of the Revised Code and working under an order authorized by one of the practitioners listed in paragraph (A)(3) of this rule, except as allowable in accordance with section 4723.01 of the Revised Code or rule 5160-27-11 of

the Administrative Code.

(5) A practitioner type described in rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code.

(6) An unlicensed practitioner who operates under general supervision, as described in rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code, of one of the practitioners listed in paragraphs (D)(1) to (D)(16) of this rule and meets the requirements for one of the following practitioner types:

(a) A qualified mental health specialist providing mental health services who:

(i) Meets the requirements for a qualified behavioral health specialist in accordance with agency 5122 of the Administrative Code,

(ii) Holds a valid high school diploma or equivalent, and

(iii) Has both work experience and training related to the service(s) being

provided.

(b) A care management specialist providing substance use disorder services who:

(i) Meets the requirements for a qualified behavioral health specialist in accordance with agency 5122 of the Administrative Code,

(ii) Is eighteen years of age or older and has a high school diploma or equivalent, and

(iii) Has an understanding of substance use disorder treatment and recovery including how to engage a person in treatment and

recovery.

(7) A pharmacist operating in accordance with rule 5160-8-52 of the Administrative

Code.

(8) A certified peer supporter operating in accordance with rule 5122-29-15.1 of the

Administrative Code.

(B) All practitioners will practice within their professional scope of practice.

(C) Supervisors will ensure that individuals whom they supervise meet the appropriate

education and training qualifications for the service(s) they render.

(D) The following practitioners operating within their scope of practice, licensure, or certification, and in accordance with any service-specific supervision requirements may supervise:

(1) Physician.

(2) Physician assistant.

(3) Certified nurse practitioner.

(4) Clinical nurse specialist.

(5) Psychologist.

(6) Board licensed school psychologist.

(7) Licensed independent social worker.

(8) Licensed professional clinical counselor.

(9) Licensed independent marriage and family therapist.

(10) Licensed independent chemical dependency counselor.

(11) Registered nurse.

(12) Licensed marriage and family therapist.

(13) Licensed chemical dependency counselor II.

(14) Licensed chemical dependency counselor III.

(15) Licensed professional counselor.

(16) Licensed social worker.

(17) Certified peer supporter as defined in rule 5122-29-15.1 of the Administrative

Code.



## 5160-27-02 - Coverage and limitations of behavioral health services



*Source: ERF 236150, PDF pages 21-26.*



Coverage and limitations of behavioral health services.

(A) This rule sets forth coverage and limitations for behavioral health services rendered to medicaid recipients by behavioral health provider agencies who meet all requirements found in agency 5160 of the Administrative Code unless otherwise

specified.

(1) All claims for behavioral health services submitted to the Ohio department of medicaid (ODM) must include an ICD-10 diagnosis of mental illness or substance use disorder. The list of recognized diagnoses can be accessed at

www.medicaid.ohio.gov.

(2) Medicaid reimbursable behavioral health services are limited to medically necessary services defined in rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code and Chapter 5160-27 of the Administrative Code. Providers shall follow the requirements in rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code and Chapter 5160- 27 of the Administrative Code regarding services that cannot be billed in

combination with other services.

(B) The following services may be subject to limitation on amount, scope, or duration of service that can be rendered to a recipient within a certain timeframe without authorization. Current and historical limitations may be found within the master list on the ODM website as described in rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code. .

(1) Individual and group therapeutic behavioral services and psychosocial rehabilitation as described in rule 5160-27-08 of the Administrative Code;

(2) Assertive community treatment (ACT) as described in rule 5160-27-04 of the Administrative Code;

(3) Mental health intensive outpatient or partial hospital programs as described in rule 5160-27-06 of the Administrative Code;

(4) Behavioral health nursing as described in rule 5160-27-11 of the administrative Code;

(5) Screening, brief intervention and referral to treatment (SBIRT) as defined by the American medical association's current procedural terminology;

(6) Psychological or neuropsychological testing;

(7) Psychiatric diagnostic evaluation with or without medical services;

(8) Psychotherapy, including family, group and multiple-family;

(9) Peer support, as described in rule 5160-27-14 of the Administrative Code. (C) The following services delivered to recipients with substance use disorders may be subject to limitations on amount, scope or duration of service that can be rendered to a recipient within a certain timeframe without authorization. Current and historical limitations may be found within the master list on the ODM website as described

in rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code.

.

(1) Substance use disorder assessment as referenced in rule 5160-27-09 of the Administrative

.

(2) Substance use disorder urine drug screening as referenced in rule 5160-27-09 of the Administrative

.

(3) Substance use disorder partial hospitalization as described in rule 5160-27- 09 of the Administrative Code. (4) Substance use disorder residential level of care as described in rule 5160-27- 09 of the Administrative Code.

(5) Substance use disorder case management as described in rule 5160-27-10 of

the Administrative Code.

(6) Substance use disorder withdrawal management as described in rule 5160-27-09 of the Administrative Code.

(7) Substance use group counseling as described in rule 5160-27-09 of the

Administrative Code.

(8) Substance use disorder intensive outpatient services as described in rule 5160-27-09 of the Administrative Code.

(D) The medications listed in appendix DD to rule 5160- 1-60 of the Administrative Code , or the fee schedule located on the ODM website are covered by ODM when rendered and billed by an eligible provider as described in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code. The medication must be administered

by a qualified practitioner acting within their professional scope of practice.

(E) Laboratory services, vaccines, and medications administered in a prescriber office

may be administered in accordance with rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code.

(F) Medical and evaluation and management services stated in the appendix to rule 5160-27-03 of the Administrative Code or appendix DD to rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code are covered by ODM when rendered by:

(1) A practitioner as described in paragraphs (A)(3) and (A)(4) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code and operating within their scope of practice; or

(2) A pharmacist, rendering services in accordance with rule 5160-8-52 of the

Administrative Code.

(G) Centers for medicare and medicaid services ( CMS ) place of service code set descriptions may be found at www.cms.gov. The department further defines place of service 99 as "community," and this place of service may only be used when a more specific place of service is not available. Place of service 99 shall not be used to provide services to a recipient of any age if the recipient is being held in a public institution as defined in 42 CFR 435.1010 ( October 1, 2023 ) nor can it be used when the actual place of service is not permissible by CMS .

(H) The activities that comprise or are included in the medicaid reimbursable behavioral health services must intend to achieve individualized goals or objectives as identified in the treatment plan and supported by a current assessment . Providers shall maintain treatment records and progress notes that provide support for medically necessary service provision as specified in rules 5160-01-27 and 5160-8-05 of

the Administrative Code.

(1) A treatment plan for mental health services may only be developed by a licensed practitioner as identified in paragraph (D)(1) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code. An unlicensed practitioner as defined in paragraph (A) (6)(a) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code may develop a treatment

plan only under the supervision of a licensed practitioner.

(2) A treatment plan for substance use disorder services may only be developed by a practitioner who, at a minimum meets the practitioner requirements found in

paragraph (A)(6)(b) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code.

(I) The medications and services listed in the appendix to rule 5160-27-03 of the Administrative Code or the opiate treatment service section of appendix DD to rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code are reimbursed by ODM when rendered and billed by an opiate treatment program as described in Chapter 5122- 40 of the Administrative Code and licensed as such by the Ohio department of behavioral health or federally certified as such as stated in 42 CFR 8.11 ( October 1, 2023 ). Reimbursement rates are determined by the methodology described in rule 5160- 4-12 of the Administrative Code or as listed in the appendix to rule 5160-27-03 of the Administrative Code or as listed in appendix DD to rule 5160-1-60 of the

Administrative Code.

(J) When permitted, provision of any service addressed in Chapter 5160-27 of the Administrative Code must comply with the appropriate telehealth requirement(s) found in rule 5160-1-18 of the Administrative Code.

For community behavioral health providers certified by the Ohio department of behavioral health, telehealth services has to also be rendered in accordance with rule 5122-29-31 of the Administrative Code.

(K) The services described in this chapter shall not substitute or supplant unpaid familial, community, vocational, or educational supports and do not include any of the following:

(1) Educational, or job training services or vocational and supported employment services unless the recipient is enrolled and receiving any of these types of services through the specialized recovery services program as

described in rule 5160-43-01 of the Administrative Code.

(2) Room and board.

(3) Habilitation services including but not limited to financial management, supportive housing, and basic skill acquisition services

that are habilitative in nature.

(4) Recreational and social activities, including but not limited to arts and crafts , game play, cosmetology, listening to music, watching television, movies, or other forms of visual media, and equine therapies;

(5) Meal or snack time;

(6) Consultation, coordination, or any collateral contact with another clinician or

colleague employed by the same organizational provider.

(7) Services that are covered elsewhere in agency 5160 of the Administrative Code; and

(8) Transportation for the recipient or family unless otherwise permitted by rule 5160- 27-13 of the Administrative Code

.

(L) The "Ohio children's initiative brief CANS assessment" and the "Ohio children's initiative comprehensive CANS assessment" are covered as defined in rule 5160- 59-01 of the Administrative Code and may be billed separately for reimbursement. Payment for therapeutic behavioral services, psychosocial rehabilitation, psychotherapy, or psychiatric diagnostic evaluation is not allowable for provision of the Ohio brief or Ohio comprehensive CANS assessment.



## 5160-27-03 - Reimbursement for community behavioral health services



*Source: ERF 236150, PDF pages 27-32.*



Reimbursement for community behavioral health services.

(A) This rule sets forth the reimbursement requirements and rates for behavioral health services as described in Chapter 5160-27 of the Administrative Code and applies to

providers as described in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code.

(B) With the exception of pharmacists as described in paragraph (A)(7) of rule 5160- 27-01 of the Administrative Code, medicaid reimbursement rates for services and practitioners described in Chapter 5160-27 of the Administrative Code are listed in the appendix to this rule. Ohio medicaid will reimburse the provider the lower of either their usual and customary charges or the reimbursement amount described in the appendix to this rule. Practitioner-specific reimbursement rates that are not otherwise stated in the appendix to this rule are determined by paragraphs (B)(1)

to (B)(5) of this rule.

(1) The reimbursement rate for physicians, as described in paragraph (A)(3) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code, is one hundred per cent of the medicaid

maximum rate stated in the appendix to this rule.

(2) The reimbursement rate for clinical nurse specialists, certified nurse practitioners, and physician assistants, as described in paragraph (A)(3) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code, is eighty-five per cent of the medicaid maximum rate stated in the appendix to this rule; except for evaluation and management office/outpatient visits, psychiatric diagnostic evaluations, and smoking and tobacco cessation counseling the reimbursement rate is one hundred per cent

of the medicaid maximum rate stated in the appendix to this rule.

(3) The reimbursement rate for practitioners described in paragraph (A)(5) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code is the reimbursement rate percentage described in rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code (medicaid maximum rate stated in the appendix to this rule). The reimbursement rates for services not defined in rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code are stated in the

appendix to this rule.

(4) The reimbursement rates for practitioners described in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code and not otherwise addressed in paragraph (B) of this rule,

are stated in the appendix to this rule.

(5) The reimbursement rate for pharmacists as described in paragraph (A)(7) of rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code is set forth in rule 5160-8-52 of the

Administrative Code.

(C) Place of service (POS) codes for behavioral health services as described in paragraph (G) of rule 5160-27-02 of the Administrative Code are stated in the appendix to this rule. If POS codes are not specified for a service, any valid POS code may be used. (D) Laboratory services, vaccines, and medications, not stated in the appendix to this rule, and administered in a prescriber office, may be reimbursed in accordance with rule 5160-1-60 of the Administrative Code.

(E) Upon implementation of rule 5160-59-08 of the Administative Code, to receive reimbursement for child and adolescent needs and strengths (CANS) assessments, the CANS assessment will be submitted in accordance with rule 5160-59-08 of the

Administrative Code.

## 5160-27-04 - Mental health assertive community treatment service



*Source: ERF 236150, PDF pages 44-53.*



Mental health assertive community treatment service.

(A) For the purposes of medicaid reimbursement, assertive community treatment (ACT) as described in rule 5122-29-29 of the Administrative Code refers to the evidence based model of delivering comprehensive community based behavioral health services to adults with certain serious and persistent mental illnesses who have not benefited from traditional outpatient treatment. The ACT model utilizes a multidisciplinary team of practitioners to deliver services to eligible recipients

.

(B) The ACT team is the sole provider of outpatient behavioral health services, including level one outpatient services as defined by the American society of addiction medicine. (C) ACT services include but are not limited to the following:

(1) Psychiatry and primary care as related to the mental health or substance use disorder diagnoses;

(2) Service coordination;

(3) Crisis assessment and intervention;

(4) Symptom assessment and management;

(5) Community based rehabilitative services;

(6) Education, support, and consultation to families, legal custodians, and significant

others who are part of the recipient's support network.

(D) A medicaid recipient may receive ACT services when determined by the ODM designated entity to have met all of the following:

(1) The recipient has a valid and current behavioral health diagnosis in

accordance with the ICD-10 diagnosis code group list found at https://bh.

medicaid.ohio.gov/manuals;

(2) The recipient has one or more of the following:

(a) A supplemental security income or social security disability insurance determination,

(b) A score of two or greater on at least one of the items in the "mental health needs" or "risk behaviors" section of the adult needs and strengths assessment (ANSA) administered by an individual with a bachelor's degree or higher and with training in the administration of the assessment; or

(c) A score of three on at least one of the items in the "life domain function" section of the ANSA which must be administered by an individual with a bachelor's degree or higher and documented with training in the administration of the assessment, and

(3) The recipient has one or more of the following:

(a) Two or more admissions to a psychiatric inpatient hospital setting during the past twelve months; or

(b) Two or more occasions of utilizing psychiatric emergency services during the past twelve months; or

(c) Significant difficulty meeting basic survival needs within the last twenty-four months; or

(d) History within twenty-four months of criminal justice involvement including but not limited to arrest, incarceration, or probation; and

(4) The recipient experiences one or more of the following:

(a) Persistent or recurrent severe psychiatric symptoms; or

(b) Coexisting substance use disorder of more than six month in duration; or

(c) Residing in an inpatient or supervised residence, but clinically assessed to be able to live in a more independent living situation if intensive services are provided; or

(d) At risk of psychiatric hospitalization, institutional or supervised residential placement if more intensive services are not available; or

(e) Has been unsuccessful in using traditional office-based outpatient services; and

(5) The recipient is eighteen years of age or older at the time of ACT enrollment. (E) ACT services may be subject to limitations on initiation and continuation of services and will be listed on the master list on the Ohio department of medicaid website as described in rule 5160-01-31 of the Administrative Code. A provider may begin submitting claims for medicaid reimbursement of ACT services for dates of service within the subsequent calendar month following the date on which prior authorization is approved by ODM or its

designee if applicable.

(F) Upon disenrollment of an ACT recipient, the ACT team shall document the circumstances regarding disenrollment in the recipient's medical record. (1) Except for services found in paragraph (O) of this rule, a recipient may not obtain behavioral health services from a provider other than the ACT team unless the recipient is disenrolled from ACT services. (G) An agency providing ACT services will hold current ACT certification given by the Ohio Department of Behavioral Health.

(1) The ACT team will be in compliance with the fidelity requirements as stated in

rule 5122-29-29 of the Administrative Code.

(2) ACT teams that employ certified peer supporters must ensure that they meet the criteria and requirements for the peer recovery support services set forth in rules 5122-29-15 and 5122-29-15.1 of the Administrative Code. (H) ODM reserves the right to suspend or terminate the payment of ACT services and to require subsequent review of an ACT team's fidelity performance if ODM has reason to believe that the ACT team's fidelity to the DACTS model described in paragraph (J)(1) of this rule may be in question. ODM may, at its discretion, suspend payment of ACT medicaid claims from the provider agency employing the ACT team until such time as ODM receives documentation from its independent validation entity that the team does meet the fidelity criteria described in paragraph (J)(1) of this rule. (I) A provider employing an ACT team may bill up to four ACT units per month per recipient when all clinical and billing requirements for each unit are met. A unit is defined as being one specific individual ACT service that is being provided. The billing of ACT units are subject to the following limits per provider category, per recipient, per month:

(1) Not more than one unit may be billed per medicaid recipient per month for services rendered by the ACT team medical prescriber including physician, clinical nurse specialist, certified nurse practitioner, or physician assistant

operating within their respective scopes of practice.

(2) Not more than one unit per medicaid recipient per month may be billed for services rendered by any one of the following ACT team members: psychologist, licensed independent social worker, licensed social worker, licensed clinical social worker, licensed professional counselor, licensed professional clinical counselor, licensed independent clinical counselor, licensed independent marriage and family therapist, licensed marriage and family therapist, licensed practical nurse, registered nurse, licensed independent chemical dependency counselor, licensed chemical dependency

counselor II or licensed chemical dependency counselor III.

(3) Not more than two units per medicaid recipient per month may be billed by an ACT team member not listed in paragraph (L)(1) or (L)(2) of this rule. This unit category includes: psychology assistant, psychology intern, psychology trainee, social worker assistant, social worker trainee, marriage and family therapist trainee, counselor trainee, chemical dependency counselor assistant, qualified mental health specialist (QMHS), including QMHS with three or more

years of experience, and peer recovery supporter.

(J) Services rendered by the ACT team medical prescriber, including physician, clinical nurse specialist, certified nurse practitioner, or physician assistant, are billable when rendered to an ACT recipient or via a case specific consultation with another member of the ACT team regarding the medical aspects of the ACT recipient's treatment plan. The ACT team medical prescriber must have at least one contact with each ACT recipient every three months. (K) When a recipient is enrolled on an ACT team, no other medicaid community behavioral health services, as defined in Chapter 5160-27 of the Administrative Code, are eligible for reimbursement except:

(1) Supported employment as identified on a recipient's specialized recovery services program treatment plan if applicable, as described in rule 5160-43-01

of the Administrative Code.

(2) Crisis services furnished by a provider other than the billing provider agency employing the ACT team.

(3) Other allowable services as described in the master list as referenced in rule

5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code.

(L) The following activities performed by members of the ACT team are not eligible for reimbursement:

## 5160-27-06 - Mental health intensive outpatient and partial hospitalization services



*Source: ERF 236150, PDF pages 57-60.*



Mental health intensive outpatient and partial hospitalization services . (A) Mental health intensive outpatient (IOP) and partial hospitalization (PHP) services are a multimodal service as described in rule 5122-29-06 of the Administrative Code. Mental health IOP and PHP are intensive, structured, goal-oriented, distinct and identifiable treatment services that address the individualized mental health needs of the recipient. Mental health IOP and PHP are clinically indicated by assessment. The environment at this level of treatment is highly structured. The purpose and intent of mental health IOP and PHP is to stabilize, increase or sustain the highest level of functioning. Recipients receiving mental health IOP or PHP will have a treatment plan that includes explicit and measurable goals that define patient improvement.

(B) For the purpose of medicaid reimbursement, this rule is applicable to the group

counseling component of IOP and PHP services.

(1) These group counseling services include but are not limited to the following:

(a) Development and strengthening of interpersonal and social competencies, including problem-solving, conflict resolution, and other skills necessary for the maintenance of functional relationships in home, school, community or vocational settings;

(b) Identification, development, and facilitation of positive coping mechanisms for the regulation and management of emotions and behavior;

(c) Managing mental health and behavioral symptoms to enhance vocational or educational opportunities or independent living skills; and

(d) Psychoeducational services including instruction and training of the recipient to increase their knowledge and understanding of their psychiatric diagnosis(es), prognosis(es), treatment, and recovery,

improve skills, and favorably affect their outcomes.

(C) Service requirements. (1) As defined in rule 5122-29-06 of the Administrative Code, mental health IOP and PHP are distinguished by treatment intensity. Treatment intensity (i.e. number of hours per week) and duration is individualized and designed to meet the needs of the recipient and will be adjusted according to the recipient's response to therapy and ability to participate effectively.

(a) Mental health IOP group counseling. Reimbursement for mental health IOP is limited to ten hours per week and may be reimbursed on an hourly basis;

(b) Mental health PHP group counseling is reimbursed as a per diem rate. Mental health PHP group counseling service provision has to meet the following criteria:

(i) Be provided for 3.5 hours or more per day; and

(ii) Be provided for a minimum of 10.5 hours per week.

(a) If at any time during the episode of care, a recipient fails

to complete the minimum weekly service requirement of 10.5 hours per week of group counseling for two consecutive weeks, any existing service authorizations will be rescinded, but services may still be reimbursed at a lower level of care not to exceed ten hours per week. (2) Mental health day IOP and PHP services has to:

(a) Be provided by a licensed practitioner as described in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code; (b) Maintain a staff to recipient ratio that does not exceed

1:12.

(D) Limitations.

(1) Reimbursement for mental health IOP and PHP services will not be made while the recipient is enrolled in assertive community treatment (ACT), or a substance

use disorder (SUD) residential treatment facility.

(2) Mental health IOP and PHP group counseling services as well as allowable same day individual and non-counseling group services, may be subject to limitations, which will be described on the master list on the Ohio department of

medicaid website as referenced in rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code.

Limitations may apply to initiation and continuation of services, frequency, scope, and duration of services, and allowances for services to be provided on the same day. (3) Mental health IOP and mental health PHP group counseling services for adults will not be reimbursed when delivered on the same day to the same recipient by any billing provider except for recipients ages eighteen or older who are enrolled in OhioRISE as referenced in rule 5160-59-02 of the Administrative Code . (E) Providers must adhere to documentation requirements set forth in rules 5160-1-27 and 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code.



## 5160-27-08 - Mental health therapeutic behavioral services and psychosocial rehabilitation



*Source: ERF 236150, PDF pages 65-70.*



Mental health therapeutic behavioral services and psychosocial rehabilitation.

(A) For the purposes of medicaid reimbursement, therapeutic behavioral services (TBS) are focused on psychoeducation and evidence-based interventions to decrease negative effects

and symptoms of mental illness.

(1) Activities included must be intended to achieve the identified goals or objectives as set forth in the individual's treatment plan. Activities include but are not limited to the following:

(a) Treatment planning. Consultation with a licensed practitioner or other eligible provider, participating in and supporting strengths-based treatment planning which may include assisting the individual and family members or other

collaterals with identifying strengths and needs.

Treatment planning should occur across the episode of care and is inclusive of planning for transition to other levels of care and dischargefrom services.

(b) Identification , provision, and ongoing monitoring of strategies or treatment options and individual progress . Assisting the individual and family members or other collaterals to identify advocate for, implement, and assess effectiveness of strategies or treatment options to address the negative effects of mental illness symptoms, emotional disturbances or associated behavioral health stressors which interfere with the individual's home, interpersonal, and

community functioning.

(c) Supportive counseling. Developing and providing solution - focused interventions and social, emotional and behavioral management skills training informed by evidence-based practices, including but not limited to:

(i) Cognitive behavioral therapy;

(ii) Dialectical behavior therapy;

(iii) Motivational Interviewing;

(iv) Trauma-informed care;

(v) Mindfulness;

(vi) Exposure therapy.

(d) Psychoeducation services. Instruction, training and ongoing support of and advocacy with individuals and family members or other collaterals to increase their knowledge and understanding of the individual's mental illness and associated symptoms, anticipated prognosis, treatment, rehabilitation, and necessary supports for community navigation and integration; and (e) Crisis prevention and amelioration. Assisting the individual with effectively responding to or avoiding identified precursors or triggers that would risk their remaining in a community setting or that result in functional impairments. Activities may include, but are not limited to, assisting the individual and family members or other collaterals with identifying a potential psychiatric or personal crisis, developing a crisis management plan, and , as appropriate, seeking other supports to restore stability

and functioning.

(2) Eligible providers must be a licensed or an unlicensed mental health practitioner in accordance with rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code who have at a minimum:

(a) A bachelor's or a master's degree in social work, psychology, nursing, or in related human services, or

(b) A high school diploma with a minimum of three years of relevant experience as determined by the employing agency and documented in

the employee's record.

(B) For the purposes of medicaid reimbursement, psychosocial rehabilitation (PSR) assists individuals with implementing interventions outlined on a treatment plan to compensate for or eliminate functional deficits and supports skills to promote independent living in a natural community environment.

(1) Activities include: (a) Restoration, rehabilitation, and support of functioning and implementation of routines and skills

critical to remaining successful in home, school, work, and community.

Activities may include:

(i) Symptom management. Assisting individuals with developing skills to minimize interference from symptoms which negatively affect daily functioning and community integration;

(ii) Independent living skills. Training and support to develop skills for completion of daily tasks necessary for independent living such as, but not limited to, budgeting, grocery shopping, personal hygiene or self-care, and managing medications;

(iii) Social skills. Improving the individual's communication, conflict resolution, and interpersonal relationship skills;

(iv) Service navigation. Providing individuals with support in learning to navigate social services and community resources;

(v) Community integration. Supporting individuals as they choose,

achieve, and maintain valued roles.

(b) Treatment planning. Consultation with a licensed practitioner or other eligible provider, participating in, and supporting strengths-based treatment planning which may include assisting the individual and family

members or other collaterals with identifying strengths and needs.

Treatment planning should occur across the episode of care and is inclusive of planning for transition to other levels of care and discharge from services.

(c) Psychoeducational services. Ongoing instruction and training of and advocacy with individuals and family members or other collaterals to increase their knowledge and understanding of the combined impact of mental illness and functional impairment and prognosis, treatment, and rehabilitation, and necessaary supports for community navigation and

integration.

(2) Eligible recipients of PSR will have a mental health diagnosis co-occurring with deficits in daily, independent, or community functioning which may be attributable to a developmental disorder, intellectual delay, cognitive

impairment or decline, medical condition, other etiology.

(3) Eligible providers must be licensed or an unlicensed mental health practitioner in accordance with rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code who are appropriately supervised in accordance with rule 5160-4-02 of the Administrative Code and have at a minimum:

(a) a bachelor's or master's degree in social work, psychology, nursing, or in a related human services field, or

(b) A high school diploma and be at least eighteen years of age, and

(c) Have completed population-specific training as determined by the

employing agency and documented in the employee's record.

(C) For the purposes of this rule, collateral contact occurs when the practitoner contacts individuals who play a significant role in a medicaid recipient's life. The information gained from collateral contactmust provide insight into the individual's treatment and

progress.

(D) Limitations.

(1) TBS and PSR will not be reimbursed when an individual is enrolled in assertive community treatment (ACT) or receiving residential substance use

disorder treatment services.

(2) Effective March 1, 2027, provision of TBS or PSR services will not be reimbursed under this rule for the provision of services which more accurately meet the description of services and activities listed in rule 5160-34-02 of the

Administrative Code.

(3) TBS and PSR may be rendered in a hospital setting only for coordinating care

related to an inpatient admission or when facilitating discharge.

(4) TBS must be delivered as an intervention with the individual and family members or other collaterals. TBS may be delivered in a group setting with the individual under the following conditions:

(a) Service provision is limited to less than 10.5 hours per week. Recipients in need of additional group services beyond this threshold should be evaluated for admission to mental health day partial hospitalization services as described in rule 5160-27-06 of the Adminstrative Code;

(b) Adults may receive up to one hour per day of TBS group on the same day as mental health day treatment intensive outpatient services provided there is documentation in the treatment plan differentiating the goals of these two services;

(c) The behavioral health provessional-to-recipient ratio of the group does not

exceed 1:12.

(5) PSR must be delivered as an individual or group intervention with the recipient. Service delivery may also occur with family members or other collaterals for the activities described in paragraphs (B)(1)(b) and (B)(1)(c) of this rule. Group PSR encounters may not exceed a 1:6 behavioral health professional to patient

ratio.

(6) TBS and PSR may be subject to limitations, which will be described on the master list on the department of medicaid website as referenced in rule 5160-1-31 of

the Administrative Code.

(E) Providers shall adhere to documentation requirements set forth in rules 5160-01-27

and 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code.



## 5160-27-09 - Substance use disorder treatment services



*Source: ERF 236150, PDF pages 75-81.*



Substance use disorder treatment services.

(A) For the purpose of medicaid reimbursement, substance use disorder (SUD) treatment services shall be defined by and shall be provided according to the American society of addiction medicine also known as the ASAM treatment criteria for addictive, substance related and co-occurring conditions for admission, continued

stay, discharge, or referral to each level of care (LOC).

(B) Medicaid will reimburse for the services provided under the following ASAM levels of care:

(1) LOC 1: outpatient services. LOC 1 services are designed to treat the individual's level of clinical severity and function. These services may be delivered in a variety of settings. Addiction, mental health, or general health care treatment personnel provide professionally directed screening, assessment, evaluation, treatment, and ongoing recovery and disease management services. Such services are provided in regularly scheduled sessions and follow a defined set of policies and procedures or medical protocols. Service provision is limited to less than nine hours per week

for adults and less than six hours per week for adolescents.

(2) LOC 2: intensive outpatient or partial hospitalization programs including LOC 2 withdrawal management (WM) LOC 2 services has to be capable of meeting the complex needs of people with addiction and co-occurring conditions.

SUD group counseling per diem services include all SUD group counseling delivered that day whether rendered continuously or in intervals throughout the day. If services are rendered non- continuously, any breaks should be documented in the recipient's record and the time elapsed to accommodate a break should not be counted toward the total billable service time. Services can occur during the day, before or after work or school, in the evening, and on weekends.

(a) LOC 2.1 intensive outpatient (IOP) requires nine to nineteen hours of SUD

group counseling service per week.

(b) The minimum length of service necessary to bill the IOP group counseling

per diem is three hours and one minute.

(c) LOC 2.5 partial hospitalization program (PHP) requires twenty or more hours of SUD group counseling service per week. If, after the first four consecutive weeks of treatment, the amount of service provided is less than twenty hours, services may still be reimbursed at a lower level of

care not to exceed 19.9 hours per week, as supported by an assessment.

(d) The minimum length of service necessary to bill the PHP group counseling

per diem is four hours and one minute.

(e) LOC 2 withdrawal management services are rendered by medical and nursing professionals who provide evaluation, withdrawal management and referral services. This level of care supports the attenuation of the physiological and psychological features of withdrawal and is inclusive of the following services:

(i) Ongoing monitoring of symptoms and assessment of progress;

(ii) Pharmacological support to manage the signs and symptoms of intoxication and withdrawal;

(iii) Presumptive urine drug screening, including specimen collection and analysis;

(iv) Venipuncture for lab testing:

(v) Non-medical support including referral to case management, counseling and involvement in community recovery support groups;

(vi) Discharge planning as described in paragraph (E)(4) of this rule that includes comprehensive education on the continuum of substance use disorder treatment, including medications for opioid use

disorder.

(f) Limitations of LOC 2:

(i) SUD per diem services cannot be billed with any other behavioral health per diem service on the same day, for the same individual,

by the same or different billing provider.

(ii) SUD group counseling is not permitted on the same day as IOP or

PHP.

(iii) Same day reimbursement for any of the following behavioral health group services must be supported by individual assessment, meet standards of medical necessity and will be limited to no more than four fifteen-minute units, or one hour per day on the same day as

the IOP or PHP group service (hourly or per diem).

(a) Mental health group psychotherapy

(b) Group therapeutic behavioral services

(c) Group behavioral health peer support

(d) Mental health day treatment

(e) Group psychosocial rehabilitation

(f) Behavioral health group nursing.

(3) LOC 3: Residential services/inpatient services including withdrawal management . These services are co-occurring capable, co- occurring enhanced, and complexity capable in nature and provided by certified or licensed addiction treatment, mental health and general medical personnel in a twenty four hour treatment setting. Services are provided in Ohio department of behavioral health certified permanent facilities which are staffed twenty four hours a day. The services listed in paragraphs (C)(3)(a) through (C)(3)(g) of this rule are included in the residential treatment service and will not be reimbursed separately: (a) Initial and ongoing assessments including diagnostic

evaluations.

, SUD level of care assessments, and SUD treatment planning assessments;

(b) Crisis intervention.

(c) Individual, group, family psychotherapy and counseling. (d) SUD case

management.

(e) Behavioral health peer recovery services. (f) Presumptive urine drug screen specimen collection and analysis; (g) Basic medical services. (h) The following services are considered non-covered for individuals in residential treatment: (i) Therapeutic behavioral services. (ii) Psychosocial rehabilitation. (iii) Mental health day treatment. (iv) Assertive community treatment. (v) Intensive home based treatment.

(i) Medicaid will reimburse a provider who is not directly affiliated with, or a staff member of, the residential treatment program for a service outside the SUD residential per diem only for an essential medically necessary service that could not be delayed until discharge. Examples include, but are not limited to:

(i) Psychiatry for the treatment of a co-occurring mental health disorder;

(ii) Specialized health care provider for treatment of an acute or chronic health condition outside a residential provider's scope of practice;

(iii) Definitive urine drug test at an offsite laboratory when necessary to finalize assessment and guide treatment decisions;

(iv) Or other necessary medical treatment that is outside the scope of the residential level of care as defined by the American society of

addiction medicine.

(j) All treatment services, regardless of provider type or provider agency, must be documented in the individual's treatment plan maintained by the residential treatment provider. If the residential treatment provider initiated the referral for an outside service, this must be documented in

the medical record along with justification for the referral.

(k) Withdrawal management services in the residential level of care must include components identified in paragraph (B)(2)(e) of this rule and be

provided within a twenty four hour supervised setting.

(l) The entity providing a residential service must ensure that the recipient has access to the appropriate provider for receipt of clinical services as stated

in the ASAM treatment criteria.

(m) Eligible practitioners of substance use disorder treatment services must meet all applicable requirements stated in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code. Qualified mental health specialists are not eligible

to be a residential treatment team practitioner.

(n) Limitations of LOC 3:

(i) Residential levels of care are mutually exclusive, therefore a recipient

can only receive services through one level of care at a time.

(ii) During level of care transitions when a recipient receives services at multiple levels of care on the same day, payment at the new level of care begins on the day following admission;

(iii) Service gaps in excess of twenty-four hours are considered a termination of one admission;

(iv) SUD residential service cannot be billed on the same day as

discharge.

(C) Medicaid will reimburse for two SUD assessment types, level of care and treatment planning, separately from a psychiatric diagnostic evaluation, for ASAM levels of

care 1, 2.1, and 2.5.

(1) Level of care assessment. The level of care assessment is a full evaluation of the ASAM dimensional criteria completed at admission and at any transition between levels of care. The purpose of this assessment is to identify clinical needs, strengths, risks, and barriers relevant to level-of-care placement and

to evaluate appropriateness for transfer or discharge based on ASAM criteria.

This assessment includes the development or update of an individualized treatment plan that includes level of care recommendation and measurable goals and interventions.

(2) Treatment planning assessment. The SUD treatment planning assessment is used to measure a recipient's progress within a level of care and is completed at regular intervals determined by program standards or when clinically indicated. This assessment includes evaluation of the recipient's response to treatment interventions, treatment progress, and barriers, and utilizes validated measurement tools when appropriate to assess change over time. The recipient's record must include documentation of the assessment

results and associated treatment plan updates.

(D) For SUD services at all levels of care, the medical record will substantiate the medical necessity and individualization of services rendered. Providers shall adhere to documentation requirements set forth in rule 5160-1-27 of the Administrative Code

and rule 5160-8-05 of the Administrative Code.

(E) Documentation of SUD services must reflect adherence to ASAM guidelines, as evidenced by the following:

(1) The patient's medical record must substantiate clinical justification for level of care placed and include documentation of ASAM dimensional risk ratings, individualized treatment goals linked to those dimensions, and evidence of

outcomes-based care.

(2) Providers will record baseline and interval scores using validated instruments appropriate to the level of care including but not limited to the "Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment of Alcohol Scale - Revised (CIWA-Ar)" for alcohol withdrawal, "Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale (COWS)" for opioid withdrawal, "Patient Health Questionnaire - 9 (PHQ-9)" for depression, and "General

Anxiety Disorder - 7 (GAD-7)" for anxiety.

(3) Progress notes must demonstrate linkage between services delivered,

dimensional needs, and measurable outcomes.

(4) Transfer or discharge readiness should be assessed ongoing by review of the recipient's progress, or lack thereof, with the individualized treatment plan goals and reviewed in comparison to the ASAM dimensions for the level of care

placement.

(F) Limitations.

(1) Twelve step or other self-led recovery support programs are not covered services

nor reimbursable by medicaid.

(2) For withdrawal management services, if there is a step-up or step-down to a different withdrawal management level of care, the consecutive billing per day

by the same organizational provider is counted as a single event.

(3) Limitations for SUD assessment and services at all levels of care will be described on the master list on the Ohio department of medicaid website as referenced in rule 5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code. Limitations may apply to initiation and continuation of services, frequency, scope, and duration of services, and allowance for services to be provided on the same day or during the same

episode of care.



## 5160-27-10 - Substance use disorder targeted case management



*Source: ERF 236150, PDF pages 82-83.*



Substance use disorder targeted case management.

(A) Targeted case management assists an individual receiving alcohol or substance use disorder treatment services from an Ohio department of behavioral health (DBH) certified substance use disorder treatment program to gain access to needed medical, social, educational

and other services.

(1) Targeted case management services shall include, at a minimum, the following activities:

(a) Comprehensive assessment and periodic reassessment of individual needs to determine the need for any medical, educational, social or other services. Assessment activities include taking client history; identifying the individual's needs and completing related documentation and gathering information from other sources such as family members, medical providers, social workers and educators to form a complete

assessment of the eligible individual.

(b) Development and periodic revision of a specific care plan that is based on the information gathered through the assessment. The care plan must include the following requirements:

(i) Goals and actions to address the medical, social, educational and other services needed by the individual; and

(ii) A plan to ensure the active participation of the eligible individual and or their authorized health care decision maker; and

(iii) A course of action to respond to the assessed needs of the eligible

individual.

(c) Referral and related activities to help the eligible individual obtain needed services

(d) Monitoring and follow-up activities or contacts that are necessary to ensure that the care plan is implemented and adequately addresses the eligible individual's needs. Changes in needs or status must be reflected in the

care plan. Monitoring shall be performed no less frequently than annually.

Monitoring may be performed in person or by electronic communication.

(B) In order to provide targeted case management, practitioners must meet the requirements in rule 5160-27-01 of the Administrative Code. For the purposes of this rule, the following unlicensed practitioners are excluded: qualified mental health

specialists and certified peer supporters.

(C) The following activities or contacts do not constitute targeted case management and are ineligible for reimbursement as targeted case management:

(1) Transportation.

(2) Waiting with an individual for appointments at social service agencies, court hearings and similar activities does not, in and of itself, constitute case

management.

(3) Direct services to which the recipient has been referred such as medical,

educational or social services.

(4) Internal quality assurance activities, such as clinical supervisory activities or

case review/staffing sessions.

(D) Targeted case management services will not be separately reimbursed when a

recipient is enrolled in a substance use disorder (SUD) residential treatment facility.

(E) SUD targeted case management services may be subject to additional limitations in scope, duration, frequency, and co-occurring services, which will be described on the master list on the Ohio department of medicaid website as referenced in rule

5160-1-31 of the Administrative Code.
